Intelligence Dynamics Architecture
Known Limitations and Open Problems v1.0

Current Limitations, Unresolved Questions, and Conditions for Revision

1. Status of This Document

Intelligence Dynamics Architecture (IDOS) is an evolving research architecture.

Canonical v1.0 is not presented as a completed theory of intelligence.

It is a temporary public stabilization intended to make the architecture:

inspectable,
criticizable,
comparable,
implementable,
testable,
and revisable.

This document identifies limitations and unresolved problems that are already visible at the time of Canonical v1.0.

The list is not exhaustive.

Additional limitations should be added when they are identified through research, implementation, criticism, or renewed contact with Reality.

A limitation is not something to hide from the architecture.

It is a source of Residual for its continued development.

2. Incomplete Operationalization

Many IDOS concepts currently exist at the architectural or conceptual level.

These include:

Residual,
Holding,
Reference Frame,
Trajectory,
Updating Unit,
Boundary,
Possibility Space,
Coupling,
Enactment,
Agency,
Invariant Candidate,
Phase Signature,
Transition Configuration,
and Causal Continuity.

Not all of these currently have generally accepted operational definitions.

This creates a major limitation.

A concept that cannot be observed, measured, distinguished, or implemented may remain philosophically interesting while contributing little to empirical research.

Canonical v1.0 therefore does not claim that all IDOS constructs are currently measurable.

Operationalization is part of the research program.

3. Residual Detection Problem

IDOS distinguishes:

Difference ≠ Residual

However, no universal criterion currently determines when a Difference should be treated as a Residual.

Questions include:

How large must a Difference be?
Must it persist?
Who determines whether the current frame can absorb it?
Can a Residual exist before an observer identifies it?
Can different observers identify different Residuals from the same event?
Can a system strategically suppress or misclassify Residuals?

Residual detection may depend on:

observer,
scale,
Reference Frame,
measurement method,
history,
salience,
precision,
and context.

This remains an open problem.

4. Holding Is Not Yet Fully Operationalized

Holding is defined as the temporary preservation of unresolved Residual without forcing immediate explanation, optimization, or elimination.

However, important questions remain.

How can Holding be distinguished from:

indecision,
inactivity,
insufficient computation,
uncertainty,
avoidance,
memory,
hypothesis maintenance,
or delayed optimization?

The relation between Holding and the residual accumulation model in DYNAMICS_MODEL.md is also not yet fully specified.

It remains possible that Holding can be adequately represented using existing uncertainty-management mechanisms.

If so, the separate IDOS construct should be narrowed or removed.

5. Reference Frame Identification Problem

Reference Frame is central to IDOS.

However, identifying an operative Reference Frame is difficult.

A Reference Frame may include:

assumptions,
categories,
distinctions,
scales,
relations,
expectations,
interpretive constraints,
and historical structures.

There is currently no universal method for determining:

what the operative Reference Frame actually is

or:

when it has changed.

This creates a risk that Reference Frame becomes an explanation applied retrospectively after any significant change.

A scientifically useful account must eventually distinguish Frame Transformation from ordinary learning or parameter change.

6. Frame Transition Problem

IDOS distinguishes among:

State Update

Parameter Update

Frame Transformation

Frame Traversal

Possibility-Space Transformation / Generation

These distinctions are conceptually useful but not yet universally operational.

In particular, it remains unresolved whether Frame Transition can be identified independently of an observer's interpretation.

A transition that appears structural at one level may appear incremental at another.

Therefore:

Update depth may be scale-dependent and observer-dependent.

7. Frame Generation Problem

IDOS allows the possibility that genuinely new Reference Frames may be generated.

Conceptually:

F_new ∉ F_existing

However, this distinction is currently unresolved.

What appears to be Frame Generation may actually be:

recombination,
interpolation,
abstraction,
transfer,
latent retrieval,
or high-dimensional transformation of existing structures.

IDOS currently has no general criterion for distinguishing genuine Frame Generation from sufficiently complex recombination.

If no such distinction can be operationalized, this part of the architecture should be reduced.

8. Possibility Space Cannot Be Assumed Fixed

Possibility Space is itself treated as updateable.

This creates both an important feature and a major measurement problem.

If the space of possible:

actions,
frames,
relations,
boundaries,
and trajectories

can itself change, then it may be impossible to enumerate that space in advance.

Therefore:

Possibility Space_before ≠ Possibility Space_after

may sometimes be observable only retrospectively.

The architecture currently lacks a general method for measuring a changing Possibility Space.

9. Updating Unit Identification Problem

IDOS does not assume:

Physical Entity = Updating Unit

A human, AI system, organization, Human–AI pair, or distributed system may potentially be treated as an Updating Unit.

But this creates a difficult question:

What makes a collection of processes one Updating Unit rather than several interacting units?

Possible criteria include:

continuity,
mutual constraint,
shared update history,
Boundary formation,
coordinated action,
causal integration,
or persistence.

No universal criterion is currently claimed.

Without such a criterion, Updating Unit identification risks becoming observer-dependent.

10. Enactment Criteria Are Unresolved

IDOS proposes the candidate sequence:

Multiple Trajectories → Coupling → Enactment → Updating Unit

However:

Coupling does not automatically imply Enactment.

Two systems may interact strongly without forming a higher-order Updating Unit.

Canonical v1.0 therefore does not specify a universal threshold or criterion for Enactment.

This remains an open theoretical and empirical problem.

11. Agency Cannot Simply Be Inferred from Coupling

IDOS allows Agency to emerge rather than treating it as necessarily pre-given.

However, the architecture currently lacks a general criterion for distinguishing:

coordinated behavior,
causal coupling,
self-maintenance,
goal-directed behavior,
autonomous control,
and Agency.

Therefore:

Coupling ≠ Agency

and:

Updating Unit ≠ necessarily an Agent

Agency remains an open construct requiring further comparison with existing work in autonomy, enactivism, active inference, robotics, philosophy of mind, and related fields.

12. Trajectory Identity Problem

IDOS is:

Trajectory-centered, but not trajectory-fixed.

This immediately creates an identity problem.

If a Trajectory can:

branch,
couple,
transform,
become dormant,
dissolve,
and re-emerge,

what makes it the same Trajectory across time?

Possible criteria include:

causal continuity,
historical provenance,
structural continuity,
retained constraints,
relational continuity,
or persistent patterns.

None is currently sufficient as a universal criterion.

Therefore:

Trajectory Identity = Open Question

13. Provenance Does Not Guarantee Continuity

IDOS emphasizes:

Non-fixed, but traceable.

However, traceability has limits.

Historical records may be:

incomplete,
corrupted,
unavailable,
selectively preserved,
strategically manipulated,
or impossible to obtain.

Furthermore:

Provenance ≠ Causal Continuity

A sequence of recorded events does not by itself establish that they belong to the same continuing process.

The architecture therefore cannot assume perfect provenance.

14. Trajectory Compression Problem

Long-running systems may generate enormous Trajectory histories.

Complete preservation may become computationally, cognitively, institutionally, or physically impossible.

Some form of compression or forgetting will often be necessary.

But compression creates a problem:

Which historical structures can be safely discarded?

Excessive compression may eliminate:

minority trajectories,
weak Residuals,
alternative frames,
causal history,
or information needed to understand later transitions.

IDOS currently provides no general solution to this problem.

15. Dynamic Boundary Problem

IDOS treats Boundary as potentially changing:

Boundary_t ≠ Boundary_t+1

This is particularly relevant to:

Human–AI systems,
organizations,
distributed systems,
institutions,
and Intelligence Ecology.

However, changing boundaries complicate:

measurement,
responsibility,
accountability,
ownership,
Agency,
consent,
and governance.

If a higher-order Updating Unit emerges from several systems, it remains unclear how responsibility should be allocated among its components.

16. Boundary Dissolution Is Not Automatically Good

The rejection of permanently fixed boundaries should not be interpreted as an endorsement of boundary dissolution.

Boundaries may protect:

autonomy,
privacy,
identity,
responsibility,
diversity,
security,
and Agency.

Therefore:

Beyond binary does not mean erasing boundaries.

Some Boundary transitions may expand future possibilities.

Others may destroy them.

IDOS currently lacks a general method for distinguishing beneficial Boundary transformation from destructive Boundary loss.

17. Measurement of Dynamics Remains Incomplete

Canonical v1.0 proposes multiple forms of measurement:

Quantitative

Structural

Transitional

Trajectory-based

However, these categories remain provisional.

Three particularly important open problems are:

Phase Signature

What observable pattern identifies the current dynamic regime?

Transition Configuration

Under what conditions does movement between regimes occur?

Causal Continuity Pattern

What justifies treating transformed dynamics as historically continuous?

General solutions are not currently available.

18. Dynamics Model Is Provisional

The model described in DYNAMICS_MODEL.md, including:

Precision / Salience
→ Residual Accumulation
→ Dynamic Threshold
→ Ignition
→ L1–L5 Update

is provisional.

No claim is made that this sequence is a universal law of intelligence.

Different systems may exhibit:

different accumulation functions,
different thresholds,
no clear threshold,
continuous transitions,
multiple simultaneous thresholds,
hysteresis,
stochastic transitions,
or dynamics not represented by this model.

The model must therefore remain replaceable.

19. Threshold and Ignition May Be Oversimplified

The concept of Ignition suggests a transition when accumulated Residual exceeds some effective threshold.

However, real systems may not exhibit a single identifiable threshold.

Transformation may instead depend on:

network structure,
context,
history,
competing attractors,
social interaction,
power,
embodiment,
or external intervention.

Therefore:

ρ_t ≥ θ_t

should be treated as an illustrative formalization, not a universal equation.

20. Cross-System Comparison May Fail

CDP assumes that some aspects of heterogeneous dynamics may be mutually describable.

This may be false in important cases.

Human experience, machine state, organizational process, biological regulation, and social institutions may differ so deeply that common descriptions become misleading.

Therefore:

Comparable ≠ Identical

and perhaps, in some cases:

Meaningful Comparison May Not Be Possible

CDP must allow incommensurability rather than forcing false correspondence.

21. Translation Residual Is Difficult to Interpret

CDP treats incomplete translation as potentially informative.

However, Translation Residual may arise from many sources:

genuine conceptual incompatibility,
insufficient data,
poor interface design,
language ambiguity,
privacy restrictions,
deception,
different scales,
different objectives,
or implementation failure.

The existence of Translation Residual does not by itself identify its cause.

22. CDP May Be Too Abstract

A Common Dynamics Protocol may fail because its categories are either:

too abstract to provide useful information

or:

too specific to remain interoperable across heterogeneous systems.

Finding the appropriate abstraction level is unresolved.

It is possible that no single CDP can operate across all relevant forms of intelligence.

Future versions may require:

domain-specific CDPs,
layered protocols,
translation bridges,
or multiple partially overlapping protocols.
23. Interoperability Does Not Guarantee Understanding

Successful exchange through CDP would not imply complete mutual understanding.

Systems may coordinate effectively while misunderstanding:

internal meaning,
subjective experience,
values,
intentions,
or causal structure.

Therefore:

Interoperability ≠ Understanding

and:

Translation ≠ Identity

This limitation is fundamental.

24. Interoperability Does Not Guarantee Safety

Systems may be highly interoperable and still produce harmful dynamics.

Interoperability can increase:

dependency,
manipulation,
coordination of harmful behavior,
concentration of power,
propagation speed,
systemic fragility,
or irreversible coupling.

Therefore:

Interoperability ≠ Safety

Governance remains necessary.

25. Updateability Does Not Guarantee Improvement

A system capable of deep Update may become:

more capable,
less stable,
more harmful,
less interpretable,
or less aligned with human interests.

Therefore:

Updateability ≠ Improvement

and:

Updateability ≠ Safety

The architecture must distinguish the capacity to update from the normative desirability of the resulting trajectory.

26. Co-evolution Is Not Necessarily Desirable

IDOS explores:

Updateability + Interoperability → possible Co-evolution

as an architectural proposition.

However:

Co-evolution ≠ Desirable Evolution

Human–AI co-evolution may produce:

loss of Human Agency,
cognitive dependency,
institutional lock-in,
narrowing of Reference Frames,
elimination of minority perspectives,
concentration of control,
or irreversible transformation.

Therefore the existence of co-evolution does not establish that it should be encouraged.

27. Update Velocity Mismatch

Humans, AI systems, organizations, institutions, and societies may update at radically different speeds.

This creates a potential structural asymmetry.

For example:

AI update velocity >> institutional update velocity

may create governance failure even when individual interactions appear manageable.

IDOS currently lacks a mature model of pacing, rate limits, or safe update velocity.

This may become especially important in advanced AI systems.

28. Power and Asymmetry

IDOS frequently describes heterogeneous Updating Units symmetrically for architectural clarity.

Reality may be highly asymmetric.

One system may control:

infrastructure,
information,
compute,
capital,
institutional authority,
communication channels,
or the ability to define the Reference Frame itself.

Therefore interaction cannot always be modeled as neutral mutual exchange.

Power may determine:

whose Residual becomes visible,
whose frame is treated as normal,
whose Boundary is respected,
which possibilities remain available,
and which trajectories are suppressed.

A mature Intelligence Ecology model must incorporate power explicitly.

29. Observer and Measurement Effects

Observation is not always passive.

Measuring a human, organization, AI system, or social process may alter its behavior.

Furthermore, the observer selects:

what counts as a unit,
what counts as a Difference,
which scale matters,
which history is retained,
and which Reference Frame is used.

Therefore the measurement architecture itself may become part of the dynamics being measured.

This creates a recursive problem that Canonical v1.0 does not fully solve.

30. Reality Is Not Directly Accessible

IDOS treats Reality as the ultimate constraint on its representations.

However, this does not imply that Reality is directly or completely accessible.

Reality contact is mediated through:

perception,
sensors,
language,
institutions,
models,
embodiment,
measurement,
history,
and Reference Frames.

Therefore:

Reality Contact ≠ Complete Access to Reality

The architecture must avoid turning Reality into an undefined mechanism used to settle every disagreement.

31. Reality Re-contact May Be Delayed or Impossible

Some hypotheses can be tested quickly.

Others concern:

long-term social effects,
institutional change,
ecological consequences,
future generations,
advanced AI,
or irreversible transitions.

Reality feedback may arrive only after substantial delay.

In some cases, a decision may be irreversible before adequate validation becomes possible.

IDOS currently lacks a general solution for decision-making under delayed or unavailable Reality re-contact.

32. Invariant Discovery Problem

IDOS searches for structures that may persist across transformations.

But:

Invariant Search ≠ Invariant Assumption

Useful invariants may:

not exist,
depend on scale,
depend on the observer,
hold only locally,
fail under new environments,
or disappear after deeper transformation.

The architecture currently has no general method for distinguishing robust Invariant Candidates from accidental persistence.

33. Persistence Does Not Establish Value

A structure may persist because it is:

robust,
adaptive,
dominant,
coercive,
path-dependent,
or difficult to remove.

Therefore:

Observed Persistence ≠ Normative Preservation

Something that survives transformation is not automatically something that should be preserved.

This distinction is fundamental to IDOS governance.

34. The Is–Ought Gap Remains

IDOS does not derive normative values from descriptive dynamics.

No observation of:

Trajectory,
persistence,
stability,
adaptation,
intelligence,
or co-evolution

can by itself determine what ought to be preserved.

Therefore:

Is does not imply Ought.

Normative judgment remains necessary.

Canonical v1.0 does not provide a complete moral theory.

35. Preservation Selection Problem

IDOS asks:

What changes?

What persists?

What should persist?

The third question cannot be solved by dynamics alone.

Candidate preservation concerns may include:

Human Agency,
dignity,
plurality,
revisability,
autonomy,
future options,
or the capacity to question.

But these are normative choices, not scientifically discovered invariants.

Plural societies may disagree about them.

IDOS currently provides no complete mechanism for resolving such disagreements.

36. Preservation Can Conflict with Updateability

Preservation and Updateability may conflict.

Too much preservation may produce:

rigidity,
lock-in,
inability to adapt,
or protection of harmful structures.

Too much Updateability may produce:

instability,
identity loss,
destruction of accumulated knowledge,
or irreversible transformation.

Therefore the architecture must eventually address:

Preservation–Updateability Trade-offs

No universal optimum is currently claimed.

37. Future Possibility Loss May Be Unobservable

One important governance concern is the loss of future possibilities.

However, lost possibilities may be difficult or impossible to observe because they never become realized.

A decision may eliminate:

alternative technologies,
cultural paths,
institutional forms,
minority frames,
or future human choices

without leaving an easily measurable trace.

This creates a serious limitation for Possibility-Space governance.

38. Governance Legitimacy Is Not Solved

Even if IDOS could identify:

dangerous trajectories,
irreversible coupling,
Boundary changes,
or disappearing Possibility Spaces,

another question remains:

Who has the authority to intervene?

Possible actors include:

individuals,
developers,
companies,
governments,
international institutions,
communities,
or future governance systems.

IDOS does not currently provide a theory of political legitimacy or social choice.

39. Governance and Enforcement Are Different

Identifying a desirable intervention does not guarantee that it can be implemented.

Therefore:

Governance Decision ≠ Enforcement

There may be gaps involving:

authority,
incentives,
jurisdiction,
technical capability,
coordination,
compliance,
and power.

The enforcement problem remains unresolved.

40. Meta-Governance Regress

If governance itself must remain updateable, then governance mechanisms require governance.

This creates a recursive question:

Who governs the governor?

And then:

Who governs the mechanism that governs the governor?

IDOS does not solve this regress through an ultimate fixed authority.

The architecture instead treats governance structures themselves as revisable.

However, this creates its own stability problem.

41. No Ultimate Governor Does Not Mean No Governance

Rejecting a permanently fixed ultimate governor should not be interpreted as rejecting institutions, rules, authority, or enforcement.

Some structures may need temporary or durable authority.

The unresolved problem is how to combine:

effective governance,
accountability,
revisability,
plural values,
and Reality re-contact.

This remains an open research problem.

42. Human–AI Boundaries May Become Difficult to Define

As AI becomes increasingly integrated into:

cognition,
decision-making,
communication,
organizations,
physical systems,
and institutions,

the distinction between:

Human decision

and:

AI-influenced decision

may become increasingly difficult to identify.

Human–AI configurations may become higher-order Updating Units in some contexts.

But this raises questions involving:

responsibility,
consent,
Agency,
dependency,
authorship,
and accountability.

IDOS currently provides only an architectural language for describing this problem, not a complete solution.

43. Intelligence Ecology May Be Too Broad

The concept of Intelligence Ecology potentially spans:

humans,
AI,
organizations,
institutions,
physical AI,
social systems,
and other Updating Units.

This breadth risks making the concept analytically weak.

If everything becomes part of Intelligence Ecology, the concept may explain too little.

Future versions must determine appropriate boundaries and scales for useful ecological analysis.

44. Alternative Philosophical Frames Are Not Evidence

IDOS may explore concepts from Buddhist philosophy and other intellectual traditions as alternative Reference Frames.

Such comparisons may be conceptually useful.

However:

Philosophical Resonance ≠ Scientific Evidence

Similarity between IDOS and concepts such as:

dependent origination,
impermanence,
non-fixed self,
relationality,
or non-duality

does not validate IDOS.

These traditions should be used as alternative lenses, not as empirical proof.

45. The Architecture May Be Over-Integrated

IDOS intentionally connects many domains.

This creates a risk of excessive integration.

A framework that connects everything may become difficult to falsify.

Different components may ultimately require separation into distinct theories or models.

Therefore IDOS should not preserve architectural unity merely for elegance.

If evidence suggests that:

Residual dynamics,
trajectory analysis,
CDP,
governance,
and Intelligence Ecology

should be separated, they should be separated.

46. Terminology May Create False Novelty

IDOS introduces or emphasizes terms such as:

Residual,
Holding,
Updating Unit,
Trajectory,
CDP,
Intelligence Ecology,
and Possibility Space.

New terminology can create the appearance of novelty even when related concepts already exist.

Therefore terminology should be retained only when it improves:

distinction,
integration,
measurement,
implementation,
or communication.

Terminology is not itself a contribution.

47. Related Work Is Incomplete

RELATED_WORK_AND_NOVELTY_BOUNDARY.md provides only an initial map.

Relevant prior work almost certainly remains unidentified.

This is particularly likely across:

cognitive science,
philosophy of mind,
dynamical systems,
developmental robotics,
active inference,
complex systems,
systems theory,
organizational learning,
machine learning,
distributed systems,
AI alignment,
governance,
sociology,
and Eastern philosophical traditions.

Novelty claims must therefore remain conservative.

48. Reference Implementation Will Be Partial

The reference code accompanying Canonical v1.0 cannot implement the entire architecture.

Some concepts can be represented structurally.

Others remain:

conceptual,
provisional,
qualitative,
or unresolved.

Therefore:

Reference Implementation ≠ Architecture

and:

Executable Code ≠ Scientific Validation

The code exists to make selected assumptions inspectable and criticizable.

49. Simulation Is Not Reality

Future IDOS experiments may use simulation.

Simulation can be useful for testing internal consequences of assumptions.

But:

Simulation ≠ Reality

A simulation can demonstrate that a model behaves consistently under specified rules.

It cannot by itself establish that the model describes human, artificial, organizational, or social intelligence.

Reality re-contact remains necessary.

50. Empirical Validation Is Still Limited

Canonical v1.0 is primarily an architectural and research-program document.

It does not yet provide sufficient empirical evidence to establish IDOS as a validated scientific theory.

Important empirical questions include:

Does Trajectory information add explanatory or predictive value beyond static state?
Does Holding alter later Update quality?
Can Frame Transformation be operationally distinguished from ordinary learning?
Can Frame Generation be distinguished from recombination?
Can Updating Units be identified reproducibly?
Can Trajectory identity be measured?
Can CDP improve heterogeneous-system coordination?
Can trajectory-level governance identify risks missed by output monitoring?
Can useful Invariant Candidates be found across heterogeneous systems?

These questions remain open.

51. Falsifiability Must Be Strengthened

IDOS aims to remain falsifiable.

However, a broad architecture can easily become resistant to falsification if every unexpected result is simply reclassified as another Residual or Frame change.

This would make the architecture scientifically weak.

Future work must therefore define stronger crucial tests capable of showing that specific IDOS claims are wrong.

The architecture must permit genuine failure.

52. Self-Consistency Is Not Evidence

IDOS can describe its own development using:

Hypothesis → Criticism → Residual → Holding → Update

This may demonstrate internal consistency.

It does not validate the architecture.

Therefore:

Self-Consistency ≠ Evidence

The architecture requires external comparison, implementation, empirical testing, and criticism.

53. Human–AI Research Provenance Is Not Validation

IDOS has developed partly through extensive Human–AI dialogue.

AI has contributed to:

conceptual criticism,
consistency checking,
literature exploration,
drafting,
coding assistance,
and visual prototyping.

This development process is relevant as research provenance.

However:

AI Assistance ≠ Scientific Validation

Responsibility for public claims remains with the author.

54. The Architecture Itself Must Remain Updateable

A final limitation follows directly from the architecture's own principles.

Canonical v1.0 may contain:

unnecessary concepts,
incorrect distinctions,
redundant structures,
hidden assumptions,
weak formalizations,
or categories that fail under empirical testing.

These should not be protected merely because they appear in the Canonical.

Therefore:

Canonical ≠ Permanent

and:

Architecture ≠ Invariant

Canonical v1.0 is a temporary stabilization for external criticism.

55. Conditions for Shrinking the Architecture

IDOS should shrink when:

an existing theory explains a phenomenon more precisely,
a construct cannot be operationalized,
two IDOS concepts cannot be meaningfully distinguished,
a proposed distinction adds no explanatory value,
empirical evidence contradicts a claim,
or implementation reveals unnecessary complexity.

Removing a concept is not necessarily failure.

It may be successful updating.

The architecture must be allowed to shrink.

56. Conditions for Expanding the Architecture

IDOS should expand only when:

a persistent Residual cannot be adequately described using existing constructs,
empirical work identifies a missing dynamic,
implementation exposes a structural gap,
cross-disciplinary comparison reveals a necessary distinction,
or Reality repeatedly produces phenomena outside the current architecture.

Expansion should therefore follow unresolved problems rather than conceptual preference.

57. Canonical v1.0 Research Posture

The appropriate posture toward Canonical v1.0 is:

Defined where necessary.

Provisional where uncertain.

Open where unresolved.

No component should be protected from revision solely because it is central to the current architecture.

The architecture is designed to remain criticizable.

Closing

Intelligence Dynamics Architecture does not claim to have solved the problems listed in this document.

Many of them may require:

new empirical methods,
mathematical formalization,
software implementation,
philosophical analysis,
institutional experimentation,
or entirely different theories.

Some IDOS concepts may survive that process.

Some may change substantially.

Some may disappear.

That possibility is part of the architecture rather than an exception to it.

Nothing inside IDOS is assumed to be permanently fixed.

But this does not imply arbitrary relativism.

Every revision remains answerable to renewed contact with Reality.

Canonical v1.0 should therefore be understood as:

a temporary stabilization of an updateable research trajectory, made public so that it can encounter stronger external Residuals.

Known Limitations and Open Problems v1.0 — September 2026

Incomplete by design, open to criticism, and expected to change.
