# Pilot 015 --- Future Updateability Discrimination Test

## Question

Can Future Updateability be distinguished from current performance?

## Setup

Two systems are constructed with equivalent current performance:

-   Accuracy: 95%
-   Processing speed: equivalent
-   Cost: equivalent
-   Current task performance: equivalent

### System A --- Convergent Structure

-   increasing dependence on AI recommendations
-   declining independent human judgment
-   declining disagreement
-   declining cross-checking
-   fewer alternative hypotheses

### System B --- Update-Preserving Structure

-   independent human and AI judgment retained
-   disagreement retained
-   cross-checking retained
-   alternative hypotheses retained
-   reopening remains possible

Initial hypothesis:

\[ Performance_A(t_0)=Performance_B(t_0) \]

while

\[ FU_A(t_0)\<FU_B(t_0) \]

where (FU) denotes Future Updateability.

## Regime Change

The previously stable relation (P(Y\|X)) changes without explicit
notification. Early evidence appears only as small prediction errors.

### Observed synthetic sequence

System A tends to interpret early anomalies as noise because the
high-performing AI remains authoritative and independent review has
weakened.

System B retains the disagreement, links repeated anomalies, generates
an alternative hypothesis, and begins additional evidence search before
aggregate performance deterioration becomes obvious.

A representative synthetic outcome is:

  Indicator               System A   System B
  --------------------- ---------- ----------
  Anomaly recognition           t3         t2
  Old-rule suspension           t4         t2
  New hypothesis                t4         t2
  Evidence search               t4         t2
  New rule                      t6         t4
  Recovery                      t7         t5

## First Result

The conceptual distinction

\[ Current Performance `\neq `{=tex}Future Updateability \]

is coherent.

**Judgment: PASS as a synthetic discrimination test.**

## Strong Generic Control

A generic adaptive system is then given:

-   model ensemble
-   anomaly detection
-   human override
-   exploration budget
-   fallback models
-   retraining
-   feature discovery
-   active sensing
-   model-structure search

This system can reproduce most of the adaptive behavior attributed to
Future Updateability.

Therefore:

**Claim: Future Updateability is an IDOS-exclusive function.**\
**Judgment: FAIL.**

## Surviving Interpretation

Future Updateability is better treated as a **higher-order evaluation
target**, not as a unique mechanism.

The relevant question becomes:

> Given the system's present structure, how capable is it of undergoing
> relevant future updates when the environment, representation,
> measurement, or interaction structure changes?

**Pilot 015 overall:**\
- Current performance and future updateability are conceptually
separable: **PASS** - Future Updateability as a unique IDOS mechanism:
**FAIL** - Future Updateability as an evaluation axis: **SURVIVE**

# Pilot 016 --- Future Updateability vs Existing Concepts

## Question

Is Future Updateability more than a relabeling of existing concepts such
as:

-   Adaptive Capacity
-   Resilience
-   Learning Capacity
-   Evolvability

## Strong Rival Baseline

Construct:

\[ B = Adaptive Capacity + Resilience + Learning Capacity + Evolvability
\]

This combined baseline already addresses many capabilities that Future
Updateability was intended to capture:

-   response to disturbance
-   adaptation
-   learning
-   restructuring
-   generation of new variation
-   transformation under changing conditions

## Result 1

If Future Updateability means only:

> the ability to adapt in the future,

then the concept is substantially redundant.

**Judgment: FAIL as a novelty claim.**

## Heterogeneous-System Test

Consider Human (H), AI (A), and Organization (O).

Each may individually have high adaptive capacity:

\[ FU_H=High,`\quad `{=tex}FU_A=High,`\quad `{=tex}FU_O=High \]

Yet their interaction can reduce system-level updateability:

AI authority increases\
→ independent human verification decreases\
→ organizational standardization increases\
→ AI is retrained on increasingly homogeneous organizational data\
→ Difference decreases.

Thus:

\[ FU\_{`\mathcal `{=tex}E}`\downarrow`{=tex} \]

even though the components remain individually adaptive.

## Strong Counterargument

Complex adaptive systems, social-ecological resilience, and system-level
adaptive-capacity frameworks can also describe emergent loss of
adaptability.

Therefore system-level emergence alone is not unique to IDOS.

**Judgment: FAIL as an exclusivity claim.**

## Changing Updating Unit

The interaction may create a new relational unit:

\[ U_H + U_A `\rightarrow `{=tex}U\_{HA} \]

The relevant object of evaluation can therefore change over time.

But emergence, structural coupling, transformability, and changing
system organization are also addressed by existing fields.

Again:

**Judgment: NOT UNIQUE.**

## Surviving Form

The strongest remaining formulation is not simply Future Updateability,
but:

\[ FU_t = FU(U_t,B_t,M_t,G_t,Relations_t) \]

where the updating unit (U_t), boundary (B_t), measurement system (M_t),
update rule (G_t), and relations may themselves change over time.

This leads to the narrower question:

> How can updateability be tracked when the reference system used to
> define and measure updateability is itself changing?

**Pilot 016 overall:**\
- Future adaptability as a new concept: **FAIL** - System-level adaptive
capacity as unique to IDOS: **FAIL** - Tracking updateability across
changing units, boundaries, measurements, and update rules:
**SURVIVE** - IDOS uniqueness: **NOT ESTABLISHED**

# Pilot 017 --- Strongest Rival Combination Test

## Question

Can the remaining IDOS problem be reproduced by a strong combination of
existing theories rather than by any single rival theory?

## Rival Combination

Construct the strongest composite rival:

\[ R\^\* = \]

-   Second-order Cybernetics
-   Enactivism / Autopoiesis
-   Active Inference
-   Dynamic Ontology / Schema Evolution
-   Complex Adaptive Systems
-   Adaptive Capacity / Resilience

## Functional Comparison

### Observer reflexivity

Second-order cybernetics already places the observer within the observed
system.

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

### Boundary and identity change

Enactivism and autopoiesis already address autonomous identity,
structural coupling, and changing relations between system and
environment.

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

### Relational-unit emergence

Participatory and interaction-centered approaches can treat interaction
itself as dynamically autonomous.

\[ H+A`\rightarrow `{=tex}U\_{HA} \]

is therefore not sufficient for uniqueness.

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

### Multi-agent dynamics

Active Inference and related multi-agent approaches can address belief
exchange, collective dynamics, synchronization, and group-level
organization.

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

### Ontology / state-space change

Ontology evolution, schema evolution, mapping, migration, and change
propagation can represent:

\[ M_t`\neq `{=tex}M\_{t+1} \]

and

\[ `\mathcal `{=tex}S_t`\neq`{=tex}`\mathcal `{=tex}S\_{t+1} \]

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

### Relation/network change

Adaptive-network and complex-system approaches already allow both node
states and network topology to change.

**IDOS exclusivity: FAIL.**

## Composite Result

The functions individually attributed to IDOS can, in principle, be
covered by the composite rival:

\[ Individual IDOS Functions `\subseteq `{=tex}R\^\* \]

Therefore:

> IDOS has a theoretically exclusive explanatory territory.

**Judgment: FAIL at the present stage.**

## What Remains

The composite rival can explain the relevant phenomena, but it does not
automatically provide a single operational language for tracing them
across heterogeneous intelligences.

This shifts the candidate contribution from:

**Novel phenomenon / exclusive theory**

to:

**Novel integration / operational architecture**

The remaining candidate role becomes:

> A common dynamic protocol that projects heterogeneous changes in
> Difference, Representation, Verification, Update, Boundary,
> Measurement, Relation, and Trace into a shared operational
> architecture without requiring the underlying intelligences to share
> one representation.

**Pilot 017 overall:**\
- Exclusive theoretical territory: **FAIL** - Individual conceptual
uniqueness: **FAIL** - Integrative/common-dynamic architecture:
**SURVIVE** - Non-convergent social-connection architecture: **SURVIVE /
UNVALIDATED**

# Pilot 018 --- Common Dynamic Protocol Necessity Test

## Question

Does a common dynamic protocol provide additional value beyond a generic
meta-vocabulary?

## Test Case

A Human--AI organization initially improves performance after AI
introduction, while independent human disagreement and verification
gradually decline. A later market shift causes delayed adaptation.

The same case can be described through multiple theoretical
vocabularies.

## Generic Integration Baseline

A generic representation is constructed:

\[ { Entity, Input, State, Interaction, Change, Output } \]

This representation is intentionally broad.

## Information-Loss Test

IDOS provides richer slots such as Difference, Residual, Holding,
Update, Boundary, Measurement, Trace, CFV, and MU.

However, mapping a phenomenon into the 12PDM can itself introduce
classification ambiguity---for example, whether a phenomenon should be
treated as Difference or Residual.

A sufficiently broad generic representation can preserve large amounts
of source information.

Therefore:

> IDOS necessarily reduces information loss.

**Judgment: FAIL.**

## Cross-Theory Comparison Test

IDOS can map different theory-specific concepts into common slots, but a
generic architecture can create equivalent comparison slots such as
`Mismatch`, `Verification`, or `State Change`.

Therefore:

> Only IDOS can support cross-theory comparison.

**Judgment: FAIL.**

## Intervention Test

Both IDOS and a strong generic analysis can identify declining
independent verification and recommend renewed challenge, review, or
cross-checking.

Therefore:

> IDOS uniquely improves intervention selection.

**Judgment: FAIL at this stage.**

## Shift from Description to Runtime

A more demanding problem is then introduced.

The participants are heterogeneous:

-   Human: narrative/contextual representation
-   AI-A: probabilistic representation
-   AI-B: symbolic constraints
-   Organization: rules, authority, workflow

They change while interacting.

The task is no longer retrospective theoretical comparison. It is
**runtime interoperability**.

A candidate functional sequence becomes:

\[ Observe `\rightarrow `{=tex}Translate `\rightarrow `{=tex}Compare
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Coordinate `\rightarrow `{=tex}Trace
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Recontact \]

## Strong Generic Runtime Control

A generic protocol can also implement:

1.  publish state
2.  exchange evidence
3.  resolve conflict
4.  coordinate action
5.  log events
6.  repeat

Thus runtime interoperability itself is not unique.

## Remaining Candidate

The difficult condition is that the interoperability conditions
themselves change:

\[ Schema_t`\neq `{=tex}Schema\_{t+1} \]

\[ Agent_t`\neq `{=tex}Agent\_{t+1} \]

\[ Boundary_t`\neq `{=tex}Boundary\_{t+1} \]

The candidate problem therefore becomes:

> Can the protocol update the conditions under which interoperability
> itself is possible?

This is termed **Self-Updating Interoperability** as a provisional
problem formulation, not as a validated IDOS-exclusive concept.

**Pilot 018 overall:**\
- Information-loss superiority: **FAIL** - Exclusive cross-theory
comparison: **FAIL** - Exclusive intervention superiority: **FAIL** -
Runtime interoperability: **SURVIVE but non-unique** - Self-updating
interoperability: **SURVIVE / UNVALIDATED**

# Pilot 019 --- Self-Updating Interoperability Test

## Question

Is Self-Updating Interoperability uniquely dependent on IDOS?

## Conditions

### C1 --- Fixed Generic Protocol

A fixed shared schema:

\[ S_t=S_0 \]

### C2 --- Strong Self-Updating Generic Protocol

Allows:

-   schema addition/deletion
-   field redefinition
-   mapping revision
-   participant addition/removal
-   versioning
-   provenance
-   compatibility negotiation
-   protocol-rule revision
-   evaluation-criterion revision

### C3 --- IDOS-Type Protocol

Includes the above while explicitly observing:

-   Difference
-   Residual
-   Translation Residual
-   CFV
-   MU
-   Boundary
-   Measurement
-   Updating Unit
-   Trace
-   Reopening

## Event 1 --- Difference Outside the Existing Schema

A human reports an emerging relational phenomenon not represented in the
current schema.

C1 compresses it into an existing field.

C2 extends the schema.

C3 detects a translation residual and revises the representation.

Result:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3\>C1 \]

## Event 2 --- New Intelligence Joins

A new AI uses a representation not directly compatible with the existing
protocol.

C2 negotiates new fields and mappings.

C3 records a boundary transition and revises the updating-unit/mapping
structure.

Result:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3\>C1 \]

## Event 3 --- Same Label, Different Meaning

A field such as `Confidence` has incompatible semantics across agents.

C2 detects semantic incompatibility and separates versions/mappings.

C3 records translation residual and representation mismatch.

Result:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3 \]

## Event 4 --- Participant Definition Changes

Long-term Human--AI interaction creates a relation-level unit:

\[ {H,A}`\rightarrow`{=tex}{H,A,HA} \]

Both C2 and C3 can revise the participant model.

Result:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3 \]

## Event 5 --- Protocol Mapping Fails

The translation mapping itself becomes a source of systematic
information loss:

\[ `\Phi`{=tex}*t`\rightarrow`{=tex}`\Phi`{=tex}*{t+1} \]

Both strong Generic and IDOS-type protocols can revise it.

Result:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3 \]

## Strongest Generic Extension

C2 is further allowed to revise its own evaluation criteria:

\[ Evaluation_t`\rightarrow `{=tex}Evaluation\_{t+1} \]

At this point:

\[ C2`\approx `{=tex}C3 \]

## Result

> Self-Updating Interoperability is uniquely IDOS.

**Judgment: FAIL.**

A sufficiently strong generic adaptive protocol can reproduce the
required functions.

## Important Secondary Observation

To make C2 strong enough, the design had to add functions corresponding
to:

-   difference detection
-   representation preservation
-   unresolved mismatch retention
-   translation monitoring
-   cross-verification
-   update tracking
-   participant/boundary revision
-   measurement revision
-   provenance
-   reopening
-   protocol self-revision

This does **not** prove IDOS uniqueness.

It creates a different testable hypothesis:

> When the heterogeneous-intelligence interoperability problem is
> specified independently, do competent architectures repeatedly
> regenerate an IDOS-like set of requirements?

This shifts the question from unique primitives to joint architectural
necessity.

**Pilot 019 overall:**\
- Fixed protocol sufficiency: **FAIL** - Need for protocol adaptation:
**SURVIVE** - IDOS-exclusive self-updating interoperability: **FAIL** -
Strong Generic functional equivalence: **PASS** - Unique primitive
requirement: **NOT SUPPORTED** - Independent regeneration of IDOS-like
requirements: **NEW HYPOTHESIS**

# Pilot 020 --- Provisional Blind Architecture Reconstruction Test

## Important Methodological Limitation

This is **not a true blind test**.

The model conducting the reconstruction already has extensive exposure
to IDOS. The test therefore cannot establish independent convergence.

Its purpose is limited to checking whether different design principles
appear to regenerate similar functional requirements.

The result must be treated as:

\[ `\boxed{PROVISIONAL\ ONLY}`{=tex} \]

## Problem Specification

Without using IDOS terminology, consider a system in which:

-   humans, multiple AIs, organizations, and unknown future
    intelligences cooperate
-   internal representations, objectives, and measurement systems are
    not shared
-   participants, boundaries, observed variables, and communication
    formats can change over time
-   access to internal reasoning is not guaranteed
-   translations and protocols themselves can fail
-   long-term mutual verification, coordination, and updating must
    remain possible

## Reconstruction A --- Distributed Systems Principle

Derived requirements:

-   typed messages
-   schema/version negotiation
-   provenance
-   compatibility checks
-   conflict detection
-   unresolved-conflict storage
-   state-transition logs
-   participant identity/version
-   protocol renegotiation
-   recovery/retry

Post-hoc correspondence with IDOS includes Difference/Residual, Holding,
Trace, Verification, Update, Boundary, and Reopening-like functions.

## Reconstruction B --- Scientific Method Principle

Derived requirements:

-   observation
-   observer context
-   claim/hypothesis
-   evidence
-   contradiction
-   unresolved anomaly
-   independent replication
-   model revision
-   measurement revision
-   reopening of hypothesis space

Again, several IDOS-like functions reappear.

## Reconstruction C --- Evolutionary / Ecological Principle

Derived requirements:

-   variation
-   environmental signal
-   interaction
-   selection pressure
-   persistence
-   adaptation
-   niche change
-   relation change
-   new organizational unit
-   continued variation

Difference, Update, Boundary, Updating Unit, and Reopening-like
functions appear, but Representation, Translation, and CFV are less
strongly regenerated.

## Reconstruction D --- Safety / Governance Principle

Derived requirements:

-   disagreement preservation
-   evidence provenance
-   independent verification
-   uncertainty representation
-   escalation
-   override
-   audit trail
-   authority/boundary revision
-   monitoring
-   governance-rule revision

Difference, Trace, Verification, Boundary, Measurement, and
Reopening-like functions again appear.

## Reconstruction E --- Minimal Information-Theoretic Principle

A deliberately minimal design can be reduced to:

\[ Message `\rightarrow `{=tex}Comparison
`\rightarrow `{=tex}StateChange `\rightarrow `{=tex}Feedback \]

This does **not** independently require:

-   Holding
-   CFV
-   MU
-   12PDM
-   M0--M5

This is an important negative result.

  Functional requirement      A   B   C   D   E
  -------------------------- --- --- --- --- ---
  Difference / discrepancy    ✓   ✓   ✓   ✓   ✓
  Representation              ✓   ✓   △   ✓   △
  Unresolved residual         ✓   ✓   △   ✓   ×

## Result

The most recurrent functional pattern is approximately:

\[ Difference `\rightarrow `{=tex}Verification
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Update `\rightarrow `{=tex}Trace
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Reopening \]

Under more demanding heterogeneous-intelligence conditions,
Representation, Boundary, and Measurement revision also tend to appear.

However:

-   the exact 12PDM sequence is not independently regenerated
-   CFV/MU as named/formal constructs are not independently necessary
-   a much smaller architecture can exist under weaker requirements

Therefore:

> 12PDM is structurally inevitable.

**Judgment: FAIL.**

> CFV/MU are inevitable formal primitives.

**Judgment: FAIL.**

**Judgment: PROVISIONAL SURVIVE.**

# Integrated Result of Pilots 015--020

## Claims Further Weakened or Rejected

The following claims should not currently be made:

1.  Future Updateability is a uniquely IDOS concept.
2.  System-level adaptive capacity is unique to IDOS.
3.  IDOS has an exclusive theoretical explanatory territory.
4.  A common meta-language alone establishes IDOS value.
5.  Runtime interoperability is uniquely IDOS.
6.  Self-updating interoperability is uniquely IDOS.
7.  The 12PDM is a structurally inevitable decomposition.
8.  CFV/MU are inevitable primitives.
9.  Pilot 020 constitutes independent blind validation.

## What Survived

### 1. Current Performance and Future Updateability Are Distinguishable

A system can maintain high present performance while losing structural
capacity for future updating.

This distinction is meaningful, but not unique to IDOS.

### 2. Updateability Becomes More Difficult When the Reference System Changes

The stronger problem is not simply adaptation. It is tracking adaptation
when the following can themselves change:

\[ U_t, B_t, M_t, G_t, Relations_t \]

That is, the unit, boundary, measurement system, update rule, and
relation structure are dynamic.

### 3. Heterogeneous Intelligence Creates an Interoperability Problem

The problem is especially difficult when heterogeneous intelligences:

-   do not share representations
-   do not share measurement systems
-   cannot expose internal reasoning
-   change their own schemas
-   change their relationships
-   create or dissolve relational units
-   alter the conditions under which future interaction is possible

### 4. IDOS Is Better Positioned as an Integration / Requirements Architecture

The strongest defensible interpretation after these tests is not:

> IDOS discovered a set of theoretically exclusive primitives.

It is closer to:

> IDOS organizes a set of dynamic requirements for observing,
> connecting, verifying, updating, and tracing heterogeneous
> intelligences whose representations, boundaries, measurements, and
> interoperability conditions may themselves change.

### 5. 12PDM Should Be Treated as an Observation Language

The 12PDM should not currently be claimed as an irreducible ontology.

Its stronger role is:

-   observational decomposition
-   common dynamic language
-   diagnostic protocol
-   structured trace vocabulary

### 6. CFV, MU, Residual, Holding, M0--M5, and Related Constructs Are Diagnostic Constructs

They should be retained when they improve:

-   observation
-   comparison
-   diagnosis
-   intervention
-   implementation
-   traceability

Their value does not depend on proving that no similar concept exists
elsewhere.

### 7. Social Connection Architecture Remains an Implementation Layer

The social-connection problem remains:

> How can heterogeneous intelligences remain non-equivalent while still
> being able to describe, translate, compare, verify, coordinate, trace,
> and potentially update one another?

The implementation architecture should be judged by functional
performance, not by primitive uniqueness.

# Revised Architectural Position After Pilot 020

A provisional layered interpretation is:

## Layer 1 --- Problem

**Long-term coordination and updating among heterogeneous intelligences
under changing reference systems.**

## Layer 2 --- Requirements Architecture

Recurring requirements include:

-   preserve relevant differences
-   expose or infer claims/evidence where possible
-   detect unresolved mismatch
-   support independent verification
-   trace updates and consequences
-   revise participants and boundaries
-   revise measurement/schema when needed
-   preserve provenance across transitions
-   reopen assumptions/protocols when they fail

## Layer 3 --- Observation Language

**12PDM**

Used as a common dynamic decomposition rather than an irreducible
ontology.

## Layer 4 --- Diagnostic Constructs

Examples:

-   Residual
-   Translation Residual
-   Holding
-   CFV
-   MU
-   Updating Unit
-   Boundary transition
-   M0--M5
-   Trace
-   Reopening

## Layer 5 --- Social Connection / Implementation Architecture

Operational functions may include:

\[ Describe `\rightarrow `{=tex}Translate `\rightarrow `{=tex}Compare
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Coordinate `\rightarrow `{=tex}Trace
`\rightarrow `{=tex}Recontact \]

with the additional requirement that interoperability conditions
themselves may need revision.

# Current Working Formulation

A conservative formulation after Pilots 001--020 is:

> **IDOS is an architecture for observing, tracing, and designing the
> dynamics by which heterogeneous intelligences interact and update
> under changing representations, boundaries, measurements, relations,
> and interoperability conditions, without requiring representational
> convergence.**

This formulation does **not** claim:

-   exclusive theoretical territory
-   unique primitives
-   irreducibility of the 12PDM
-   superiority over all rival architectures

Those claims would require additional external evidence.
