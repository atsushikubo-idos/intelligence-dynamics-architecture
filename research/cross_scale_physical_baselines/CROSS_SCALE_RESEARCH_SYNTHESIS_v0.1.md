# Cross-Scale Research Synthesis — From Atomic Bonding to IDOS (v0.1)

## Scope and scientific discipline
This research sequence starts with established physics and chemistry. It then develops separate, **synthetic**, testable comparisons for IDOS. The social conclusions are **not deductions from quantum mechanics**. This note summarizes previously reported v0.4–v1.6 studies and the freshly re-executed v1.7 audit. Earlier files/results were not re-run in creating this synthesis; their numerical details require independent reproduction from their own archived code. The integration remains a research hypothesis, not a validated cross-domain law.

## Two tracks that must not be conflated

**Physics and chemistry track (v0.4–v0.8):**
- v0.4: H2/H2+ electronic-structure energy curves using minimal basis methods; equilibriums are not actual time-dependent reaction formation.
- v0.5: finite-basis full configuration interaction (FCI) vs restricted Hartree–Fock and H2 bond dissociation; FCI is exact within the chosen basis, not in the continuum.
- v0.6: classical nuclear motion on an FCI-derived potential energy curve, plus an artificial damping term; damping is not real photon emission or dust-mediated chemistry.
- v0.7: explicit finite oscillator environment with approximately conserved total energy. Transient capture and energy backflow, not proof of irreversible molecule production.
- v0.8: sensitivity to environment coupling, oscillator count, frequency distribution. The finite bath is a simplified proxy rather than a molecular formation rate model.

**Predictive/relationship track (v0.9–v1.7):**
- v0.9: formation, persistence, and loss of third-party mediated relationships under synthetic consent and transfer rules.
- v1.0: automated selection of a future-relevant, pre-defined history feature; standard models perform similarly.
- v1.1: discovery of predictive order patterns from anonymous synthetic event sequences.
- v1.2: hidden-state inference using HMM on an HMM-like generator; favorable model class by construction.
- v1.3: three observers' event histories and relational sequence features; the same features allow standard models similar accuracy.
- v1.4: change detection, model retraining, and variation of observation, relationship and environmental rules; detection is not causal attribution.
- v1.5: different underlying causes with indistinguishable observed likelihoods; a stipulated diagnostic separates them only under limited hypotheses.
- v1.6: Bayesian choice among pre-defined diagnostic actions with information, cost, burden and refusal constraints; existing Bayesian decision methods provide the same choice.
- v1.7: observation/diagnostic action has its own transition effect on relationship survival; information-only and risk-aware policies have a tradeoff, under known synthetic probabilities.

## Cross-domain correspondence is a hypothesis, not a conservation law
| Physical/chemical problem | Candidate IDOS question | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|
| Potential energy minimum | Conditions that permit a connection | No social energy function was derived |
| Nuclear trajectory and encounter | Actual formation vs mere possibility | Human/AI interaction dynamics not measured |
| Coupling to finite environment | Third-party effects and persistence | No physical analogy directly predicts consent or trust |
| Coarse-graining | Which historical distinctions support future prediction? | Provenance/CFV/MU advantage over standard sufficiently informed models |
| Backflow and finite-time capture | Apparent stability vs longer-term persistence | Real-world time scales and risk identification |
| Measurement and intervention | Data collection can change the target system | Empirical intervention kernel and autonomy safeguards |

## Reproducibility hierarchy
1. **Computational repeatability:** fixed algorithms, parameters, seeds, and tests. v1.7 has this.
2. **Model validity:** demonstrate that dynamics and observables accurately approximate a defined target. Not established for social models.
3. **Independent generalization:** unseen tasks and different environments, including strong baselines. Only synthetic evaluations so far.
4. **Causal/real-world validation:** controlled data or natural experiments with ethical constraints. Not established.

## What is genuinely useful now
- Clear distinction between possible, formed, persistent, and reconfigurable structures.
- Quantified effects of retaining or discarding predictive history.
- Formal limits to observational identifiability; abstention is appropriate when causes are indistinguishable.
- Consent/refusal and measurement burden expressed as hard constraints rather than optimizing only diagnostic accuracy.
- Provenance and reference-frame revision can be specified as measurable software requirements, not assumed novel physics.

## Next research gate
Freeze the proliferation of synthetic examples. Audit the completeness and interdependencies of v0.4–v1.7; define a single preregistered benchmark with (i) multi-actor partial observation, (ii) ethical non-intervention option, (iii) observed and changed intervention kernels, (iv) held-out environments, (v) complete-information and Bayesian/POMDP baselines, and (vi) explicitly justified metrics for future updateability, provenance integrity, and relationship persistence. Only then evaluate whether the integration offers new performance or a distinct systems-engineering contribution.
