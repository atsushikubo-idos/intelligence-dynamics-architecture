# 1. Case Title

**Amazon Proteus — Human-to-Robot Natural-Language Delegation**

---

# 2. Regime Classification

$$
\boxed{V003 = Transition}
$$

V003 represents a system in which Human / AI / Robot roles, operational authority, interpretation, and execution boundaries are actively shifting.

The core transition is:

$$
Human\ specifies\ operation
\rightarrow
Human\ specifies\ intent
$$

and:

$$
Robot\ executes
\rightarrow
Robot\ interprets + plans + executes
$$

This case is therefore used to test whether IDOS becomes more useful when the boundary between human intention and machine execution is itself changing.

# 4. Stage 1 — Raw Reconstruction

## 4.1 What happened?

Amazon expanded its autonomous mobile robot **Proteus** from a system focused on autonomous movement toward a broader system capable of receiving higher-level human instructions and translating those instructions into operational behavior.

Earlier Proteus deployments emphasized autonomous navigation and safe operation in shared spaces.

The next-generation design expands this structure toward:

- broader facility movement;
- natural-language interaction;
- task interpretation;
- priority handling;
- route selection;
- timing decisions.

Conceptually:

$$
Human\ Intent
\rightarrow
Natural\ Language\ Instruction
\rightarrow
Robot\ Interpretation
\rightarrow
Priority + Route + Timing
\rightarrow
Physical\ Action
$$

---

## 4.2 Natural-language delegation

A major change is the interaction model.

Rather than requiring:

$$
Technical\ Command
$$

or:

$$
Programming\ Interface
$$

the system is designed to accept:

$$
\boxed{
Natural\ Language
}
$$

from employees.

The human provides what should be achieved.

Proteus then determines important aspects of how the task should be executed.

---

## 4.3 Change from earlier Proteus

Earlier structure:

$$
Assigned\ Task
\rightarrow
Autonomous\ Navigation
$$

Next-generation structure:

$$
Human\ Intent
\rightarrow
Robot\ Interpretation
\rightarrow
Task\ Planning
\rightarrow
Physical\ Execution
$$

Thus machine autonomy expands from:

$$
How\ to\ move
$$

toward:

$$
How\ to\ interpret\ and\ execute\ the\ task
$$

---

## 4.4 Human role

Humans remain responsible for setting the high-level objective.

The structural shift is from:

$$
Human
\rightarrow
Specific\ Operational\ Instruction
$$

toward:

$$
Human
\rightarrow
Goal / Intent
$$

while:

$$
Robot
\rightarrow
Operational\ Decomposition
$$

This does not establish that humans are reduced to supervisors in every operational context.

It only shows that some operational decomposition is delegated to the robot.

---

## 4.5 Robot role

Proteus expands beyond autonomous navigation into:

- instruction interpretation;
- task prioritization;
- route selection;
- timing;
- physical execution.

Thus:

$$
Execution\ Autonomy\uparrow
$$

and potentially:

$$
Interpretive\ Autonomy\uparrow
$$

---

## 4.6 Embodied system

Proteus is an embodied autonomous system operating in a physical environment with:

- humans;
- carts;
- inventory;
- equipment;
- other robotic systems.

AI interpretation therefore produces:

$$
Physical\ Action
$$

rather than only digital output.

---

## 4.7 Deployment status

Proteus itself is an operational Amazon robotics platform.

Amazon has also described next-generation natural-language capabilities and expanded deployment.

Public reporting does not establish that every next-generation capability is deployed at equal maturity across all locations.

Therefore deployment claims are interpreted conservatively.

---

## 4.8 Broader robotics direction

Proteus is consistent with Amazon’s broader direction toward robotics systems that can:

- understand natural language;
- reason over tasks;
- act autonomously.

This suggests a structural progression:

$$
Instruction
\rightarrow
Interpretation
\rightarrow
Autonomous\ Action
$$

---

## 4.9 Actors and components

Relevant components include:

- frontline employee;
- Proteus;
- natural-language interface;
- perception system;
- navigation system;
- task-planning logic;
- fulfillment center;
- carts and inventory;
- other robots;
- operational software.

A key addition relative to V002 is:

$$
\boxed{
Intent\ Translation
}
$$

between human instruction and robot action.

---

## 4.10 Decision / execution structure

V002:

$$
System\ State
\rightarrow
Fleet\ Coordination
\rightarrow
Robot\ Movement
$$

V003:

$$
Human\ Goal
\rightarrow
Natural\ Language
\rightarrow
AI\ Interpretation
\rightarrow
Task\ Planning
\rightarrow
Robot\ Execution
$$

The machine therefore contributes not only to execution, but also to operational interpretation.

---

## 4.11 Authority structure

A useful raw distinction is:

$$
Goal\ Authority_H
$$

versus:

$$
Operational\ Authority_R
$$

Humans retain high-level goal authority.

Robots receive greater authority over execution details.

No evidence establishes that Proteus independently determines its own goals.

---

## 4.12 Temporal structure

The robot interprets, plans, and acts without human approval of every micro-step.

Thus the human role shifts away from:

$$
Step\text{-}by\text{-}step\ control
$$

toward:

$$
Goal\ specification
+
exception\ oversight
$$

---

## 4.13 What was observed?

Public evidence directly supports:

- autonomous movement;
- operation in shared human spaces;
- expanded natural-language interaction;
- robot-side handling of priority, route, and timing;
- broader operational scope.

---

## 4.14 What remains unknown?

Public evidence does not fully establish:

- how ambiguous natural-language instructions are resolved;
- how misunderstandings are detected;
- where humans can intervene;
- whether user correction changes future model behavior;
- the internal task-planning algorithm;
- the criteria used for priority assignment;
- conflict handling between multiple human instructions;
- conflict handling between multiple robots;
- provenance of robot interpretation;
- long-run effects on human operational roles.

These remain open.

V003 is strongly explainable through established frameworks.

The most direct are:

- Shared Autonomy;
- Supervisory Control;
- Adjustable Autonomy;
- Delegation Theory;
- Human–Robot Interaction;
- Embodied AI;
- Intent Recognition;
- Safety Engineering;
- Human Factors.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Conventional\ analysis\ remains\ very\ strong
}
$$

---

## 5.2 Shared Autonomy

The system can be represented as:

$$
Human\ Goal
\rightarrow
Robot\ Planning
\rightarrow
Robot\ Execution
$$

This is a standard shared-autonomy structure.

---

## 5.3 Supervisory Control

Humans move away from continuous micro-level control and toward high-level supervision.

The robot handles:

- route;
- timing;
- planning;
- execution.

The human handles:

- goal;
- constraints;
- exception handling.

---

## 5.4 Adjustable Autonomy

The case can also be described as a shift along an autonomy continuum:

$$
Manual
\rightarrow
Assisted
\rightarrow
Supervised
\rightarrow
Autonomous
$$

This already captures much of the observed transition.

---

## 5.5 Delegation Theory

The structure can be described as:

$$
Human:\ What\ to\ achieve
$$

$$
Robot:\ How\ to\ achieve
$$

Thus:

$$
Goal\ Authority
\neq
Execution\ Authority
$$

This is not uniquely IDOS-specific.

---

## 5.6 Human–Robot Interaction

Existing HRI can address:

- instruction ambiguity;
- intent recognition;
- trust;
- predictability;
- explainability;
- exception handling.

---

## 5.7 Embodied AI

Proteus operates in the physical world.

Thus:

$$
Language
\rightarrow
Semantic\ Interpretation
\rightarrow
Operational\ Plan
\rightarrow
Physical\ Action
$$

is already a conventional embodied-AI problem.

---

## 5.8 Safety Engineering

Natural-language interpretation remains constrained by physical safety requirements.

Conventional safety engineering remains directly relevant.

---

## 5.9 Human Factors

Potential issues include:

- trust;
- complacency;
- expectation mismatch;
- mode confusion;
- misunderstanding of system capability.

These are established human-factors concerns.

---

When:

\neq

across multiple layers.

### C. Semantic oversight

$$
\boxed{

}

### E. Correction vs learning

$$

\neq

$$

### F. Formal vs effective authority

$$
Formal\ Authority
\neq
Effective\ Authority
$$

### G. Meaning becomes operational infrastructure

Interpretation itself becomes part of the system that converts human intent into physical action.

# 6. Stage 3 — IDOS Analysis

## 6.1 Updating Unit

V003 makes the Updating Unit less stable.

Candidates include:

- Human;
- Robot;
- Human–Robot relation;
- delegation relation.

The strongest candidate is:

$$
\boxed{
U \approx Human\text{-}Robot\ Delegation\ Relation
}
$$

The relation itself becomes a plausible unit of update.

---

## 6.2 Dynamic Boundary

V003 activates Dynamic Boundary strongly.

Functions previously located on the human side include:

- task decomposition;
- priority setting;
- route selection;
- timing.

Some of these move toward the robot side.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
B_t \neq B_{t+1}
}
$$

Unlike V002, this is not only a question of where the analyst draws the boundary.

The operational boundary itself is shifting.

---

## 6.3 Trajectory

The transition can be represented as:

$$
Human\ specifies\ action
\rightarrow
Human\ specifies\ task
\rightarrow
Human\ specifies\ intent
\rightarrow
Machine\ interprets + plans + executes
$$

The key trajectory is not simply:

$$
Autonomy\uparrow
$$

but:

$$
Interpretive\ Authority_R\uparrow
$$

---

## 6.4 Difference

V003 makes Difference more consequential.

The central difference is:

$$
\boxed{
Human\ Intent
\neq
Robot\ Interpretation
}
$$

This is better understood as a translation difference than as a simple execution error.

---

## 6.5 Reference Frame

Humans and robots operate with different representational frames.

Human frame may include:

- context;
- tacit knowledge;
- operational nuance;
- implicit priorities.

Robot frame may include:

- machine-interpretable constraints;
- perceptual state;
- map;
- task representation;
- operational rules.

Thus:

$$
F_H \neq F_R
$$

The system therefore contains:

$$
\boxed{
Reference\ Frame\ Translation
}
$$

---

## 6.6 Measurement

Task completion is not sufficient.

V003 introduces the distinction:

$$
Execution\ Success
\neq
Interpretation\ Success
$$

and therefore:

$$
Task\ Completion
\neq
Intent\ Fidelity
$$

This makes measurement design more central.

---

## 6.7 Provenance

V003 requires more than action logs.

The relevant chain is:

$$
Human\ Instruction
\rightarrow
Interpretation
\rightarrow
Planning
\rightarrow
Execution
$$

This suggests the importance of:

$$
\boxed{
Semantic\ Provenance
}
$$

---

## 6.8 Future Updateability

The second-order question becomes:

$$
\boxed{
Can\ the\ organization\ later\ reallocate\ interpretive\ authority?
}
$$

Natural-language delegation may increase human convenience while reducing direct operational practice.

The issue is therefore not only skill retention but future ability to redesign who interprets what.

---

## 6.9 CFV

V003 separates:

$$
Action\ Verification
$$

from:

$$
Interpretation\ Verification
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Action\ Verification
\neq
Interpretation\ Verification
}
$$

---

## 6.10 MU

V003 also separates immediate correction from durable update.

$$
MU_{H\rightarrow R}^{action}
$$

is distinct from:

$$
MU_{H\rightarrow R}^{model}
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Correction
\neq
Learning
}
$$

---

## 6.11 Verification ≠ Influence ≠ Update

A more detailed distinction is required:

$$
Verification
\neq
Influence
\neq
Operational\ Update
\neq
Model\ Update
$$

---

## 6.12 Power

V003 strengthens the Power dimension.

The key question becomes:

$$
\boxed{
Who\ gets\ to\ operationalize\ meaning?
}
$$

Humans may retain formal goal authority while robots gain operational interpretive authority.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Formal\ Authority
\neq
Effective\ Interpretive\ Authority
}
$$

---

## 6.13 Temporal Compatibility

Robot interpretation and action may occur faster than continuous human review.

However, V003’s core issue is less the speed difference itself than the shift in interpretation and authority.

Thus temporal compatibility remains relevant but is not the central differentiator.

---

## 6.14 Reality Contact

Reality Contact remains strong because machine interpretation leads to physical action.

However, this is not the main source of incremental visibility in V003.

---

## 6.15 Residual

V003 gives Residual a more natural role:

$$
Human\ Intended\ Meaning
-
Robot\ Interpreted\ Meaning
$$

This can be treated as:

$$
\boxed{
Translation\ Residual
}
$$

---

## 6.16 Holding

Holding could matter if the robot preserves ambiguity and requests clarification rather than acting immediately.

Public evidence does not establish such a mechanism.

Therefore:

$$
Holding = plausible,\ not\ evidenced
$$

---

## 6.17 Possibility Space

Robot-side planning increases the range of possible operational plans.

This is useful but not central to V003.

---

## 6.18 12PDM

V003 increases the relevance of high-resolution observation of representation and translation.

However, the core analysis remains possible without full 12PDM use.
