# Intelligence Dynamics Architecture
## Glossary v1.0

This glossary defines the core terms used in Intelligence Dynamics Architecture (IDOS).

The purpose is not to claim universal definitions of intelligence-related concepts.

These definitions specify how the terms are used within IDOS Canonical v1.0.

Epistemic status:

- **[D] Defined** — defined for use within the architecture.
- **[P] Provisional** — a candidate mechanism or theoretical proposition.
- **[O] Open** — an unresolved research question.

A definition within IDOS does not by itself imply scientific validation.

---

## 1. Reality [D]

**Definition**

Reality refers to that with which an Updating Unit, observer, system, or architecture remains in contact and against which its representations, expectations, actions, and updates encounter constraint.

Reality is not assumed to be completely or directly accessible.

Access to Reality may be mediated by observation, embodiment, instruments, language, institutions, history, scale, and Reference Frames.

Nevertheless, IDOS assumes that renewed contact with Reality may generate Differences that current representations or structures fail to absorb.

**Not**

- a complete internal model,
- a Reference Frame,
- whatever an observer currently believes,
- or a claim that Reality can be observed without mediation.

**Core relation**

> **Everything remains answerable to Reality.**

---

## 2. Observation [D]

**Definition**

Observation is a process through which some aspect of Reality becomes available to an Updating Unit or observer.

Observation is always potentially selective and frame-dependent.

What becomes observable may depend on:

- sensors,
- embodiment,
- instruments,
- categories,
- scale,
- history,
- attention,
- and Reference Frame.

**Not**

- complete access to Reality,
- necessarily passive perception,
- or a frame-independent representation.

---

## 3. Difference [D]

**Definition**

A Difference is a distinction, mismatch, contrast, change, or deviation that becomes available through observation or interaction.

Not every Difference becomes a Residual.

A Difference may be fully absorbed by the currently operative structure.

**Not**

- automatically an error,
- automatically meaningful,
- or automatically a Residual.

---

## 4. Residual [D/P]

**Definition**

A Residual is a Difference that cannot be sufficiently absorbed by the currently operative representation, expectation, Reference Frame, organization, or updating process.

Residuals may be:

- locally absorbed,
- ignored,
- suppressed,
- accumulated,
- held,
- reinterpreted,
- or become resources for deeper Update.

**Not**

- simply prediction error,
- necessarily numerical,
- necessarily noise,
- or something that must always be eliminated.

**Core distinction**

> **Difference ≠ Residual**

A Difference becomes relevant as a Residual only relative to an operative structure that cannot sufficiently absorb it.

---

## 5. Precision [P]

**Definition**

Precision refers to the estimated reliability, confidence, or evidential quality associated with an observed Difference or Residual.

A large mismatch with low reliability may have less dynamic influence than a smaller but highly reliable contradiction.

Its operational definition may vary across systems.

**Not**

- a universally specified numerical quantity within current IDOS,
- or equivalent to Salience.

---

## 6. Salience [P]

**Definition**

Salience refers to the contextual importance or relevance of a Difference or Residual to an Updating Unit, Trajectory, Reference Frame, Boundary, task, or situation.

**Not**

- necessarily magnitude,
- necessarily Precision,
- or a universally defined scalar.

---

## 7. Holding [P]

**Definition**

Holding is the temporary preservation of unresolved Residual without forcing immediate explanation, optimization, elimination, or closure.

Holding allows unresolved Difference to remain dynamically available while additional Reality contact, interpretation, interaction, or transformation occurs.

**Not**

- inactivity,
- permanent indecision,
- simple storage,
- or refusal to Update.

The operational implementation of Holding remains open.

---

## 8. Update [D/P]

**Definition**

Update refers to a transformation in response to Reality contact, Difference, Residual, interaction, or accumulated history.

Update may affect:

- state,
- parameters,
- models,
- Reference Frames,
- Boundaries,
- Updating Units,
- Trajectories,
- or Possibility Spaces.

**Not**

- necessarily optimization,
- necessarily improvement,
- or necessarily learning within a fixed frame.

An Update may be beneficial, harmful, reversible, irreversible, local, or structural.

---

## 9. Updateability [D/P]

**Definition**

Updateability is the capacity of a system, relation, Trajectory, or Updating Unit to remain capable of revision through continued contact with Reality.

It includes the possibility that not only state but also Reference Frame, Boundary, or Possibility Space may become revisable.

**Not**

- continuous change for its own sake,
- maximum adaptability,
- or absence of stability.

---

## 10. Trajectory [D/P]

**Definition**

A Trajectory is the historically structured path produced through sequences of interaction, Difference, Residual, Holding, Update, action, persistence, and transformation.

Conceptually:

> **Trajectory = Change + Persistence + History**

This is conceptual shorthand, not a mathematical equation.

Trajectory is central to IDOS because current state alone may not capture how a system became what it is.

**Not**

- merely a sequence of states,
- a permanently fixed identity,
- or necessarily the property of a predefined Agent.

**Core principle**

> **Trajectory-centered, but not trajectory-fixed.**

Trajectories may emerge, couple, branch, transition, become dormant, dissolve, and re-emerge.

What constitutes continuity of the "same" Trajectory remains an open research question [O].

---

## 11. Provenance [D/P]

**Definition**

Provenance is the traceable historical record through which a Trajectory, representation, decision, transformation, or structure can be related to prior states and events.

Provenance supports investigation of continuity without requiring permanent identity.

**Not**

- guaranteed complete history,
- perfect causal reconstruction,
- or an invariant that must always survive transformation.

**Core principle**

> **Non-fixed, but traceable.**

---

## 12. Coupling [D/P]

**Definition**

Coupling is a relation in which two or more Trajectories or processes become mutually constraining, mutually responsive, or dynamically interdependent.

Conceptually:

**{T₁, T₂, ... Tₙ} → Coupling**

**Not**

- automatically cooperation,
- automatically interoperability,
- automatically Agency,
- or automatically a new Updating Unit.

Coupling may be weak, temporary, asymmetric, harmful, productive, or transformative.

---

## 13. Enactment [P/O]

**Definition**

Enactment refers to a process through which sufficiently organized relations among trajectories or processes become meaningfully describable as a higher-order Updating Unit, Boundary, Reference Frame, or form of Agency.

Conceptually:

**Multiple Trajectories → Coupling → Enactment → Updating Unit**

No universal criterion for Enactment is currently claimed.

**Not**

- mere interaction,
- simple aggregation,
- or automatically evidence of a new Agent.

---

## 14. Updating Unit [D/P]

**Definition**

An Updating Unit is a temporarily identifiable organization of processes whose updates exhibit sufficient continuity, coupling, or mutual constraint to be meaningfully observed as a unit.

**Core distinction**

> **Physical Entity ≠ Updating Unit**

One physical entity may contain multiple relevant updating processes.

Multiple physical entities may also participate in a higher-order Updating Unit.

Examples may include, depending on context:

- an individual human,
- an AI system,
- a Human–AI configuration,
- a team,
- an organization,
- or another dynamically organized system.

These examples do not imply that every such entity automatically constitutes one Updating Unit.

**Not**

- necessarily a physical individual,
- necessarily permanent,
- or necessarily equivalent to an Agent.

---

## 15. Boundary [D/P]

**Definition**

A Boundary is the temporarily operative distinction through which processes, states, histories, or interactions are treated as inside or outside a relevant Updating Unit or organization.

Boundaries may influence:

- what counts as internal or external,
- what becomes a Residual,
- attribution of action,
- responsibility,
- available interaction,
- and Trajectory identity.

**Not**

- necessarily physical,
- necessarily permanent,
- or necessarily binary.

Conceptually:

**Boundary(t) ≠ Boundary(t+1)**

**Core principle**

> **Beyond binary does not mean erasing boundaries.**

---

## 16. Agency [P/O]

**Definition**

Agency refers to the capacity attributed to an organized process or Updating Unit to participate in action, selection, intervention, or trajectory-shaping interaction.

IDOS does not necessarily assume Agency as a primitive starting point.

It investigates whether Agency may emerge through dynamic organization, coupling, and Enactment.

**Not**

- automatically implied by interaction,
- automatically implied by Coupling,
- or necessarily identical to physical individuality.

A general operational criterion for Agency is not yet specified.

---

## 17. Reference Frame [D]

**Definition**

A Reference Frame is a temporarily operative structure of distinctions, assumptions, relations, categories, scales, and interpretive constraints through which an Updating Unit encounters, organizes, and interprets Reality.

A Reference Frame influences:

- what becomes observable,
- what becomes meaningful as Difference,
- what may be recognized as Residual,
- what is ignored,
- what counts as successful Update,
- what Boundaries become visible,
- and what actions or possibilities become intelligible.

**Not**

- Reality itself,
- merely a state variable,
- necessarily a complete world model,
- necessarily a conscious belief,
- or a permanently fixed worldview.

**Core distinction**

> **Reference Frame ≠ Reality**

A Reference Frame may itself be transformed, traversed, recombined, destabilized, generated, or dissolved.

---

## 18. Frame Transformation [P]

**Definition**

Frame Transformation is a change in the operative structure through which Reality is interpreted.

Conceptually:

**F(t) → F(t+1)**

The system changes more than parameters inside an otherwise stable frame.

**Not**

- merely Parameter Update,
- or necessarily movement to an already available alternative frame.

---

## 19. Frame Traversal [P]

**Definition**

Frame Traversal refers to movement between distinguishable Reference Frames that are already available or accessible.

Conceptually:

**Fᵢ → Fⱼ**

**Not**

- necessarily Frame Generation,
- or simply modifying parameters inside one frame.

---

## 20. Possibility Space [D/P]

**Definition**

A Possibility Space is the temporarily available structure of states, actions, interpretations, Reference Frames, relations, or Trajectories that can be generated, considered, explored, or enacted from a given configuration.

Possibility Space is not assumed to be fixed.

Changes in Trajectory, Boundary, Reference Frame, or interaction may alter what becomes possible or even imaginable within the system.

**Not**

- simply a list of known choices,
- necessarily static,
- necessarily fully observable,
- or identical to a Reference Frame.

**Core distinction**

> **Reference Frame structures interpretation.  
> Possibility Space structures what may become available as an alternative.**

---

## 21. Possibility-Space Transformation / Generation [P/O]

**Definition**

Possibility-Space Transformation refers to a change in the structure of available possibilities.

A stronger candidate process, Possibility-Space Generation, refers to the emergence of possibilities not adequately represented within the previously operative space.

Conceptually:

**PossibilitySpace(t) → PossibilitySpace(t+1)**

A stronger formulation is:

**F(new) ∉ F(existing)**

Whether genuinely new Possibility Spaces can be operationally distinguished from sufficiently complex recombinations of existing structures remains open [O].

---

## 22. Invariant Candidate [P/O]

**Definition**

An Invariant Candidate is a structure, relation, constraint, or pattern hypothesized to remain sufficiently robust across specified transformations.

Possible transformations include:

- State change,
- Reference Frame change,
- Boundary change,
- scale change,
- observer change,
- environmental change,
- or Trajectory transition.

**Not**

- an assumed eternal truth,
- automatically universal,
- or automatically normatively valuable.

**Core principle**

> **Invariant Search ≠ Invariant Assumption**

---

## 23. Preservation [D/P]

**Definition**

Preservation refers to the descriptive or normative process of retaining some structure, capacity, value, possibility, history, or condition across transformation.

IDOS distinguishes:

> **Observed Persistence ≠ Normative Preservation**

Something that persists is not therefore something that should persist.

Preservation decisions themselves remain open to Reality contact, plural contestation, consequence, and revision.

---

## 24. Interoperability [D/P]

**Definition**

Interoperability is the capacity of heterogeneous Updating Units to observe, translate, compare, coordinate, or interact across differences in internal representation, embodiment, history, values, timescale, and Reference Frame.

**Not**

- representational identity,
- value homogenization,
- or forced internal unification.

IDOS investigates:

> **Dynamic Interoperability without Representational Unification.**

---

## 25. Common Dynamics Protocol — CDP [P]

**Definition**

The Common Dynamics Protocol (CDP) is a candidate descriptive and interoperability layer for representing and relating the dynamics of heterogeneous Updating Units without requiring their internal representations to become identical.

Candidate descriptive dimensions may include:

- Reality Contact,
- Difference,
- Residual,
- Holding,
- Update,
- Trajectory,
- Boundary,
- Reference Frame,
- Coupling,
- transition,
- and provenance.

The detailed specification of CDP remains under development.

**Not**

- a claim that all intelligences share identical internal architecture,
- a universal programming protocol,
- or a mechanism that causes Coupling.

**Core distinction**

> **Coupling ≠ CDP**

Coupling is a phenomenon.

CDP is a candidate protocol for describing or supporting interoperability across heterogeneous dynamics.

---

## 26. Co-evolution [P]

**Definition**

Co-evolution refers to a process in which interacting Updating Units or Trajectories change not only independently but through their continuing influence on one another.

Conceptually:

**(A(t), B(t), Relation(t))  
→  
(A(t+1), B(t+1), Relation(t+1))**

IDOS investigates the proposition that continued Updateability and Interoperability may support such dynamics.

**Not**

- automatically beneficial,
- automatically symmetric,
- or automatically safe.

> **Co-evolution ≠ Desirable Evolution**

---

## 27. Intelligence Ecology [P/O]

**Definition**

Intelligence Ecology refers to a field of interacting and potentially co-evolving Trajectories, Updating Units, Couplings, Boundaries, Reference Frames, and Possibility Spaces across heterogeneous forms of intelligence.

Possible participants may include:

- humans,
- AI systems,
- physical AI,
- Human–AI configurations,
- organizations,
- institutions,
- and larger social systems.

**Not**

- merely a collection of fixed Agents,
- or necessarily a harmonious system.

A central question is:

> **What trajectories are heterogeneous intelligences creating in one another?**

---

## 28. Dynamic Threshold [P]

**Definition**

A Dynamic Threshold is a provisional construct representing conditions under which accumulated Residual can no longer be sufficiently absorbed by the currently operative mode of Update.

Conceptually:

**ρ(t) ≥ θ(t)**

The threshold itself may depend on history, Reference Frame, Boundary, context, resources, or Trajectory.

No universal threshold is currently claimed.

---

## 29. Ignition [P]

**Definition**

Ignition is a provisional term for a transition in which accumulated dynamics contribute to movement from one mode or depth of Update to another.

Conceptually:

**Accumulated Residual → Threshold Condition → Ignition → Deeper Update**

**Not**

- necessarily catastrophic failure,
- a universal law,
- or a fully operationalized mechanism.

---

## 30. Update Levels L1–L5 [P]

IDOS provisionally distinguishes five depths of Update.

### L1 — Local Absorption

Residual is handled locally without material transformation of the operative Reference Frame.

### L2 — Parameter Update

Parameters change within a substantially stable Reference Frame.

### L3 — Frame Transformation

The operative Reference Frame itself changes.

### L4 — Frame Traversal

The Updating Unit moves between distinguishable Reference Frames.

### L5 — Possibility-Space Transformation / Generation

The structure of available Reference Frames or possibilities itself changes.

These levels are not rankings of intelligence.

A deeper Update is not automatically better.

---

## 31. Phase Signature [O]

**Definition**

A Phase Signature is a candidate observable pattern that may help identify the current dynamic regime of a Trajectory or Updating Unit.

No general operational definition currently exists.

---

## 32. Transition Configuration [O]

**Definition**

A Transition Configuration is a candidate description of the conditions under which a Trajectory, Boundary, Reference Frame, Updating Unit, or Possibility Space moves from one regime to another.

Possible variables may include Residual history, Coupling, Boundary state, environmental conditions, and prior Trajectory.

No universal configuration is currently claimed.

---

## 33. Causal Continuity Pattern [O]

**Definition**

A Causal Continuity Pattern is a candidate structure used to investigate whether a transformed, dormant, branched, dissolved, or re-emergent process may meaningfully be treated as continuous with an earlier Trajectory.

It is intended to address the question:

> **What makes a Trajectory the same Trajectory across transformation?**

No universal answer is currently claimed.

---

## 34. Intelligence [P/O]

IDOS does not currently define intelligence as a fixed substance, score, capability, or property possessed by a permanently predefined Agent.

Its central working proposition is:

> **Intelligence is a Trajectory.**

More precisely, IDOS investigates intelligence as a historically structured and potentially relational process through which states, frames, boundaries, possibilities, and forms of Agency may remain capable of transformation through continued contact with Reality.

This proposition remains open to empirical and conceptual challenge.

---

# Core Relations

The central vocabulary can be compressed into the following relations:

**Reality  
→ Observation  
→ Difference  
→ Residual  
→ Holding  
→ Update  
→ Trajectory**

**Multiple Trajectories  
→ Coupling  
→ Enactment  
→ Updating Unit**

**Updating Unit  
↔ Boundary  
↔ Reference Frame  
↔ Possibility Space**

**Updateability + Interoperability  
→ possible Co-evolution**

**Individual Trajectory  
→ Relational Trajectory  
→ Intelligence Ecology**

And beneath all of them:

> **Nothing inside the architecture is assumed to be permanently fixed.**

while:

> **Everything remains answerable to Reality.**
