# Research Update — ver.79

## Relational Updating Units, Non-Reducibility, and Measurement

**Date:** September 26, 2026  
**Status:** Exploratory Research Extension

This document records an ongoing extension of the Intelligence Dynamics Operating System (IDOS).

It does not replace the canonical architecture established in ver.78.

The purpose of ver.79 is to extend the concept of the Updating Unit from a conceptual construct toward a testable and measurable research object, especially in Human–AI relational systems.

The central question is:

> When does a persistent Human–AI interaction become a relational Updating Unit rather than merely an interaction between two separate components?

---

The primary contribution of ver.79 is not a revision of the core dynamics.

It is an extension of the question:

**What is the unit in which these dynamics occur?**

---

## 2. Interaction Is Not Sufficient

A Human–AI interaction does not automatically constitute an Updating Unit.

Let:

- H = Human
- A = AI
- C_HA = Human–AI coupling
- U_HA = Relational Updating Unit

Then:

(H, A, C_HA) ≠ U_HA

Interaction alone is insufficient.

A single conversation, tool invocation, or episodic collaboration should not automatically be interpreted as the emergence of a higher-order Updating Unit.

This creates a new research problem:

**What conditions are necessary to identify a relational Updating Unit?**

---

## 3. Candidate Detection

ver.79 separates the detection of a candidate Updating Unit from its empirical validation.

A provisional candidate may be represented as:

(H, A, C_HA, M_HA, B_HA) → U*_HA

where:

- C_HA = Persistent Coupling
- M_HA = Relational Memory
- B_HA = Relational Boundary
- U*_HA = Candidate Relational Updating Unit

### 3.1 Persistent Coupling

The interaction must persist across time.

The output or state of one component must repeatedly affect the subsequent state of the other.

A simplified relational sequence may be represented as:

H_t → A_t → H_(t+1) → A_(t+1) → ...

The relevant phenomenon is not frequency alone, but recurrent mutual influence.

### 3.2 Relational Memory

A persistent relation may accumulate structures that affect future updating.

Examples include:

- shared conceptual vocabulary
- accumulated distinctions
- rejected hypotheses
- unresolved Residuals
- previous Frame changes
- historical decision paths

This relational memory is represented as:

M_HA

The key question is whether the history of the relation constrains future updating in ways that cannot be adequately described as isolated memory states of H and A.

### 3.3 Relational Boundary

A measurable Updating Unit requires an operational boundary.

B_HA

The boundary specifies what is included in the candidate system and what remains outside it.

Possible components may include:

- Human
- AI
- conversational history
- documents
- code
- external tools
- institutional constraints
- other agents

Without an operational boundary, the Updating Unit risks expanding indefinitely and losing explanatory usefulness.

---

## 4. Candidate Detection Is Not Validation

The existence of persistent coupling, relational memory, and a definable boundary does not prove that a relational Updating Unit exists.

Therefore:

Candidate Detection ≠ Validation

The first stage asks:

**Is there a sufficiently persistent relational structure to justify further investigation?**

The second stage asks:

**Does this relation exhibit dynamics that cannot be adequately reduced to its individual components?**

This distinction is intended to reduce circularity in the Identity Criterion.

---

## 5. Non-Reducibility as a Testable Hypothesis

Non-reducibility should not be assumed as part of the definition of an Updating Unit.

It should be treated as an empirical hypothesis.

### 5.1 Possibility Space

Let:

- P_H = Possibility Space generated by the Human
- P_A = Possibility Space generated by the AI
- P_HA = Possibility Space generated through the Human–AI relation

The null hypothesis is:

H0: P_HA = P_H ∪ P_A

The alternative hypothesis is:

H1: P_HA ≠ P_H ∪ P_A

The research question is whether the relation generates possibilities that cannot be sufficiently reconstructed from the isolated possibility spaces of H and A.

### 5.2 Trajectory

Let:

- τ_H = Human trajectory
- τ_A = AI trajectory
- τ_HA = Relational trajectory

The null hypothesis is:

H0: τ_HA = τ_H + τ_A

The alternative hypothesis is:

H1: τ_HA ≠ τ_H + τ_A

The question is whether the long-term trajectory of the relation contains persistent structure that cannot be adequately explained as the aggregation of individual trajectories.

---

## 6. Validation Strategy

Possible validation methods include:

- independent baseline estimation
- holdout data
- counterfactual comparison
- reconstruction tests
- ablation
- cross-period replication
- cross-task replication
- independent model evaluation

The objective is not to demonstrate that relational emergence must exist.

The objective is to create conditions under which the non-reducibility hypothesis could fail.

If individual-level models sufficiently reconstruct P_HA and τ_HA, the relational Updating Unit hypothesis may not be necessary for that case.

If they systematically fail, while a relational model provides reproducible explanatory or predictive improvement, evidence for a relational Updating Unit becomes stronger.

## 7. Relational Measurement

Traditional measurement may focus on:

Measurement(H)

and:

Measurement(A)

ver.79 introduces an additional possibility:

Measurement(U_HA)

Candidate relational variables include:

P_HA = Relational Possibility Space

τ_HA = Relational Trajectory

C_HA = Coupling structure

M_HA = Relational Memory

B_HA = Relational Boundary

L_HA = Relational Limit

The purpose is not merely to create independent numerical scores for each variable.

The more important target is the temporal structure of transitions among processes.

---

## 8. Three Levels of Measurement

A provisional measurement architecture can be divided into three levels.

### Level 1 — Detection

Does a process occur?

For process i:

x_i(t) ∈ {0,1}

This allows basic identification of events such as:

- Residual formation
- Holding
- Update
- Limit manifestation
- Enactment

### Level 2 — Intensity

How strongly or persistently does the process occur?

Possible indicators include:

- frequency
- duration
- recurrence
- information change
- influence on subsequent states

For example, accumulated Residual may provisionally be represented as:

ρ_t = Σ_k w_k |Res_(t-k)|

This remains a research construct rather than a finalized metric.

### Level 3 — Dynamics

The primary IDOS measurement target is not an isolated variable but the transition structure across time.

The canonical sequence remains:

Reality
→ Difference
→ Representation
→ Residual
→ Holding
→ Update
→ Possibility Space
→ Trajectory
→ Limit
→ Enactment
→ Reality'
→ Reopening

The research question becomes:

**How does an Updating Unit move through these processes over time?**

---

## 9. Asymmetric Coupling

Human–AI coupling should not be assumed to be symmetric.

Represent coupling as:

C_HA = (C_(H→A), C_(A→H))

In general:

C_(H→A) ≠ C_(A→H)

Potential measurement dimensions include:

- direction of influence
- strength of influence
- authority to modify Frames
- authority to modify Memory
- information-access asymmetry
- control over system boundaries
- dependency
- lock-in
- freedom of Exit

This makes it possible to study relational intelligence without assuming equality between the participating components.

---

## 10. Updating Unit, Subject, and Sovereign Subject

ver.79 explicitly separates three concepts:

Updating Unit ≠ Subject ≠ Sovereign Subject

An Updating Unit is a unit in which persistent updating dynamics can be observed.

This does not imply that the unit is a Subject.

Therefore:

U_HA ⇏ Subject_HA

Similarly, the existence of subject-like properties does not automatically imply sovereignty:

Subject_HA ⇏ Sovereign Subject_HA

The observation of relational emergence therefore does not itself establish:

- personhood
- rights
- sovereignty
- moral status
- legal responsibility

These questions belong to a separate layer of Normative Deliberation.

---

## 11. Relational Limit

ver.79 also introduces the possibility that Limit may exist at the relational level.

Let:

L_H = Human Limit

L_A = AI Limit

L_HA = Relational Limit

A Human–AI relation may allow one component to overcome limitations that would remain unresolved individually.

However, the relation may also generate new shared Frames, dependencies, blind spots, or structural constraints.

Therefore, a relational Updating Unit may simultaneously:

- expand the accessible Possibility Space
- overcome some individual Limits
- generate new relational Limits

This creates an important future measurement problem.

---

## 12. Scale Extension

The Updating Unit should not necessarily be fixed at the level of the individual.

Possible scales include:

X → U_ij → U_ijk → U_social

where these may approximately correspond to:

Individual → Relation → Organization / Team → Society / Institution

The broader hypothesis is that similar updating dynamics may appear at multiple scales.

The relevant empirical question is therefore not only:

**How does an intelligent subject update?**

but also:

**At what scale does a persistent and measurable updating dynamics emerge?**

---

## 13. Research Significance of ver.79

The central shift in ver.79 can be summarized as:

Interaction
→ Candidate Detection
→ Validation
→ Relational Measurement
→ Scale Extension

This changes the status of the Updating Unit.

It is no longer only a conceptual answer to the question:

**What updates?**

It becomes an empirical research problem:

**Can the Updating Unit be detected, distinguished, measured, and falsified?**

The central methodological principle is:

**Do not assume the Updating Unit in advance.**

Instead:

**Detect it. Test it. Measure it.**

## 14. Open Research Questions

The following questions remain open:

1. How should P_H, P_A, and P_HA be operationally estimated?

2. What constitutes sufficient evidence for non-reducibility?

3. How should relational memory M_HA be distinguished from aggregated individual memory?

4. How should Boundary B_HA be identified without arbitrary observer dependence?

5. Can L_HA be inferred from repeated Residual and failed updating?

6. How should asymmetric coupling be quantified?

7. Can the same measurement architecture be applied across individuals, Human–AI relations, organizations, and social systems?

8. Can independent observers or models reliably identify the same Updating Unit?

9. Does relational measurement improve prediction or explanation compared with individual-level models?

10. Under what conditions does a relational Updating Unit emerge, persist, transform, or dissolve?

These questions define the next stage of empirical and computational development.
