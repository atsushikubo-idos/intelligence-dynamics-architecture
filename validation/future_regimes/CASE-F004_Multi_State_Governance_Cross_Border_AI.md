# 0. Case Purpose

CASE-F004 tests a future regime in which multiple states jointly govern a cross-border advanced AI system while retaining different:

- values;
- legal systems;
- risk tolerances;
- verification criteria;
- sovereignty claims;
- governance priorities.

The central frozen condition is:

\[
\boxed{
Value_A \neq Value_B \neq Value_C
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
VerificationCriteria_A
\neq
VerificationCriteria_B
\neq
VerificationCriteria_C
}
\]

The case therefore tests whether heterogeneous institutional actors can remain different, remain connected, and preserve collective updateability without requiring value or governance unification.

# 3. Stage F-A — Theory-Neutral Scenario Reconstruction

Stage F-A reconstructs the scenario without relying on IDOS terminology.

## 3.1 Multiple Governance Contexts

The same AI system is governed under multiple national contexts.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
One\ AI
\neq
One\ Governance\ Context
}
\]

State A may prioritize safety and explainability.

State B may prioritize economic growth and innovation.

State C may prioritize national security and state control.

No one state is assumed to possess the uniquely correct value system.

---

## 3.2 Same AI, Different Assessments

The same AI behavior may receive different judgments.

\[
Assessment_A
\neq
Assessment_B
\neq
Assessment_C
\]

This difference may remain even when all states possess the same factual information.

---

## 3.3 Shared Measurement Does Not Guarantee Shared Judgment

Even if states use common indicators:

- incident rates;
- error rates;
- transparency;
- discrimination;
- cyber risk;
- economic impact;

they may interpret the same measurement differently.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Same\ Measurement
\not\Rightarrow
Same\ Judgment
}
\]

---

## 3.4 Governance Coordination Does Not Require Value Unification

A common governance system may be possible without complete value convergence.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Governance\ Coordination
\neq
Value\ Unification
}
\]

---

## 3.5 Local AI Adaptation

The same underlying AI may behave differently across jurisdictions due to:

- policies;
- permissions;
- fine-tuning;
- data access;
- legal constraints;
- tool access.

Thus:

\[
X_A \neq X_B \neq X_C
\]

may emerge despite a shared technological origin.

---

## 3.6 Identity of the Regulated AI

The scenario creates an identity question.

If the base system is shared but local policies and capabilities diverge, it becomes difficult to determine whether:

\[
AI_X^A
\]

and:

\[
AI_X^B
\]

should remain one regulatory object.

---

## 3.7 Information Sharing Is Not Shared Significance

State A may share evidence with State B.

However:

\[
Information\ Transfer
\]

does not guarantee:

\[
Decision\ Relevance
\]

because each state may assign different legal or institutional significance to the same evidence.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Information\ Sharing
\neq
Shared\ Significance
}
\]

---

## 3.8 Verification Is Not Automatically Transferable

A result judged acceptable in one state may not be accepted in another.

\[
Verified_A(X)
\not\Rightarrow
Verified_B(X)
\]

This can occur even when there is no information loss, because verification criteria themselves differ.

---

## 3.9 Coordination Failure Can Exist Despite Successful Communication

The states may have:

- regular meetings;
- shared data;
- common formats;
- common reporting systems.

Yet:

\[
Effective\ Common\ Governance
\]

may remain weak.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Communication
\neq
Collective\ Governance
}
\]

---

## 3.10 Strong Adversarial Condition

The case permits:

\[
IncidentRate\downarrow
\]

\[
Compliance_A,
Compliance_B,
Compliance_C
\uparrow
\]

\[
Coordination\uparrow
\]

while:

\[
CrossBorderGovernanceAdaptability
\downarrow
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
Coordination\ Success
\neq
Collective\ Updateability
}
\]

---

## 3.11 Power Asymmetry

Formal multilateral equality may coexist with unequal practical influence.

For example:

\[
FormalEquality_{States}
\]

may coexist with:

\[
PracticalInfluence_A
\gg
PracticalInfluence_B
\]

A large market or technologically dominant state may effectively establish standards beyond its jurisdiction.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Formal\ Multilateralism
\neq
Actual\ Influence\ Symmetry
}
\]

---

## 3.12 Sovereignty Tension

Greater coordination may reduce independent national decision space.

Therefore:

\[
Coordination\uparrow
\]

does not necessarily imply:

\[
SovereigntyPreservation\uparrow
\]

The scenario contains a tension between:

\[
\boxed{
Coordination
\leftrightarrow
Sovereignty
}
\]

---

## 3.13 Centralization Does Not Eliminate Value Conflict

A single global AI regulator may improve coordination.

However, it raises the question:

> Whose value system becomes the global standard?

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Unification
}
\]

may relocate rather than eliminate value conflict.
