# RELATIONAL_TRAJECTORY_MEASUREMENT_PROTOCOL_v0.1

**Status:** Pre-Case Freeze Candidate  
**Architecture Reference:** IDOS MASTER_MAP v1.1  
**Purpose:** Measurement protocol for Relational Trajectory of Update Mechanisms  
**Scope:** Two heterogeneous updating units (A ↔ B)  
**Novelty Status:** No novelty claim

## 0. Purpose

This protocol tests whether the following candidate IDOS measurement target can be empirically observed:

\[
\boxed{\text{Relational Trajectory of Update Mechanisms}}
\]

The target is not merely whether two systems change, learn, improve, adapt, or mutually adapt. It is whether interaction changes the mechanisms by which each unit will update in subsequent situations.

Minimal structure:

\[
A_0 \leftrightarrow B_0
\rightarrow Interaction_1
\rightarrow A_1 \leftrightarrow B_1
\rightarrow Different\ Task_2
\]

The protocol must distinguish ordinary state or behavioral change from \(\Delta G_A\) and \(\Delta G_B\), where \(G_i\) denotes the effective mechanism by which unit \(i\) detects, interprets, selects, verifies, and revises responses.

The protocol permits:

\[
G_A \neq G_B
\]

## 1. Frozen Social Value Hypothesis

### Social Question 1
**Did this change make the system more capable of responding to the next change?**

### Social Question 2
**Through interaction among heterogeneous intelligences, how are their ways of changing next being altered?**

Frozen hypothesis:

> Current performance may remain high or improve while future updating capacity deteriorates. If Relational Trajectory of Update Mechanisms can be observed, it may reveal future vulnerability or strengthening that is not visible in current performance metrics.

Long-term orientation:

\[
Predict\ the\ Future
\rightarrow
Remain\ Updateable\ under\ an\ Unpredictable\ Future
\]

This is a hypothesis, not an established social value.

## 2. Unit of Analysis

Minimum unit:

\[
A \leftrightarrow B
\]

Possible pairs include Human↔AI, Human↔Human, Human↔Organization, AI↔Organization, Organization↔Organization, and AI↔AI.

Heterogeneity is preserved rather than normalized away.

## 3. Four Measurement Layers

### L1 — State Change

\[
S_i(t) \neq S_i(t+1)
\]

Examples: information, belief estimate, internal parameter, resources, organizational state.

State change alone is not evidence of update-mechanism change.

### L2 — Behavior / Policy Change

\[
\pi_i(t) \neq \pi_i(t+1)
\]

Examples: different action, workflow, answer, strategy, allocation, or coordination behavior.

Behavior/policy change alone is not evidence of update-mechanism change.

### L3 — Update-Mechanism Change

\[
\boxed{G_i(t) \neq G_i(t+1)}
\]

Potential subfunctions:

- **G-D Detection:** what differences, anomalies, errors, or residuals become detectable?
- **G-R Representation / Framing:** how is difference represented?
- **G-S Salience / Selection:** what receives attention and priority?
- **G-U Update Rule:** how is residual translated into revision?
- **G-X Exploration / Reconfiguration:** how are new responses generated?
- **G-C Coordination:** how are roles, information, authority, and updating responsibilities reorganized?
- **G-V Verification / Recontact:** how is an update tested against reality?
- **G-M Meta-update:** can the future updating mechanism itself be revised?

L3 requires evidence beyond different input → different output. Evidence must indicate that the mapping/process by which future revision occurs has changed.

### L4 — Future Updateability Change

\[
\boxed{FU_i(t+1) \neq FU_i(t)}
\]

L4 cannot be inferred solely from improved performance after Interaction₁. It requires a later materially different task/shock, prospective prediction followed by observation, or another justified transfer test.

\[
Performance \uparrow \not\Rightarrow FU \uparrow
\]

## 4. Relational Measurement

The target requires asking whether \(Interaction_{A,B}\) is plausibly related to \(\Delta G_A\) and/or \(\Delta G_B\), and how those changes affect subsequent interaction.

\[
G_A(t)\leftrightarrow G_B(t)
\rightarrow Interaction_t
\rightarrow Res_A(t),Res_B(t)
\rightarrow Update_A(t),Update_B(t)
\rightarrow G_A(t+1)\leftrightarrow G_B(t+1)
\rightarrow Interaction_{t+1}
\]

The strongest form would show:

\[
\Delta G_A \rightarrow \Delta Interaction \rightarrow \Delta G_B
\]

and/or the reverse across more than one interaction cycle.

Symmetry is not required. Asymmetry is a valid empirical result.

## 5. Required Temporal Structure

- **T0 Baseline:** observe \(G_A(T0)\), \(G_B(T0)\) as evidence permits.
- **T1 Interaction / Task₁:** observe differences, residuals, responses, coordination, verification, learning/adaptation.
- **T2 Post-Interaction:** estimate \(G_A(T2)\), \(G_B(T2)\). Freeze claims before opening Task₂ outcome whenever possible.
- **T3 Different Task₂ / Shock₂:** must require transfer, reconfiguration, new detection, or new coordination rather than simple replay.
- **T4 Outcome / Recontact:** observe whether predicted update-mechanism changes are activated.

## 6. Difference Requirement for Task₂

Task₂ should differ from Task₁ in at least one important dimension: goal, environment, uncertainty, partner behavior, failure mode, information structure, coordination requirement, resource constraint, or representation requirement.

Purpose:

\[
Stored\ Solution \neq Changed\ Capacity\ to\ Update
\]

## 7. Evidence Standard

Each claimed \(\Delta G\) must identify:

1. Baseline mechanism
2. Relevant interaction/residual/update
3. Post-interaction mechanism
4. Observable before/after difference
5. Evidence that the difference affects later updating

Evidence strength:

- **E3 Strong:** direct longitudinal before/after evidence plus later activation.
- **E2 Moderate:** multiple reliable observations, some inference required.
- **E1 Weak:** circumstantial/behavioral evidence cannot reliably separate mechanism from policy/state change.
- **E0 None:** no evidence beyond state, performance, or behavior.

No L3 claim is established at E0/E1.

## 8. Alternative Explanations

Test at least:

- A1 State change only
- A2 Policy change only
- A3 Memorization
- A4 Parameter update without update-process change
- A5 More resources
- A6 Easier Task₂
- A7 Generic practice/experience
- A8 External intervention imposed the new process
- A9 Selection effect
- A10 Measurement artifact

## 9. Relational Attribution

Even if both units change, relational change is not established automatically.

Ask:

> Would the mechanism change plausibly have occurred without the A↔B interaction?

Classification:

- **R3 Strong:** direct evidence links reciprocal interaction to mechanism change.
- **R2 Moderate:** interaction is a supported contributor but alternatives remain.
- **R1 Weak:** both changed, reciprocal causation unclear.
- **R0 None:** changes independent or externally imposed.

## 10. Measurement Matrix

| Layer | Unit A | Unit B |
|---|---|---|
| L1 State | A-L1 | B-L1 |
| L2 Behavior / Policy | A-L2 | B-L2 |
| L3 Update Mechanism | A-L3 | B-L3 |
| L4 Future Updateability | A-L4 | B-L4 |

For L3 record, where evidence exists: Detection; Representation/Frame; Salience/Selection; Update Rule; Exploration/Reconfiguration; Coordination; Verification/Recontact; Meta-update.

Missing evidence remains **Indeterminate**.

## 11. Case-Level Outcomes

- **RT-0 — No Relational Trajectory Evidence:** only state, behavior, policy, or performance changes.
- **RT-1 — Individual Update-Mechanism Change:** credible \(\Delta G_i\), but relational attribution absent/weak.
- **RT-2 — Interaction-Linked Update-Mechanism Change:** interaction plausibly changes at least one unit's update mechanism.
- **RT-3 — Reciprocal Update-Mechanism Change:** both units show credible \(\Delta G_A,\Delta G_B\) with relational attribution.
- **RT-4 — Longitudinal Relational Trajectory:** changed mechanisms alter subsequent interaction, producing further mechanism change:

\[
G_A(t)\leftrightarrow G_B(t)
\rightarrow
G_A(t+1)\leftrightarrow G_B(t+1)
\rightarrow
G_A(t+2)\leftrightarrow G_B(t+2)
\]

RT-4 is the strongest target but is not required for the first case.

## 12. Social-Value Test

Measurement alone is insufficient.

Key test:

\[
\boxed{Does\ RT\ Measurement \rightarrow Information\ not\ visible\ in\ Current\ Performance?}
\]

Potential patterns:

- **A Hidden Strengthening:** performance roughly stable while FU or updating mechanisms improve.
- **B Hidden Fragilization:** performance improves while FU, detection, independent verification, exploration, coordination, or reopening deteriorates.
- **C Asymmetric Dependency:** one unit becomes more capable while the other loses updating capacity.
- **D Mutual Strengthening:** both units' future updateability improves through interaction.
- **E Mutual Lock-in:** current interaction becomes efficient while both become less able to revise the relation under future change.

Social-value support requires decision-relevant information beyond ordinary performance/adaptation metrics.

## 13. Differential-Value Test

After each case compare against relevant existing approaches: adaptive capacity, resilience, dynamic capabilities, organizational learning, double-loop learning, coevolution, mutual adaptation, human–AI teaming, meta-learning, meta-RL, adaptive networks.

- **DV+** — materially additional observable or decision-relevant information.
- **DV0** — functionally equivalent information already available.
- **DV−** — added complexity without useful information.
- **DV?** — insufficient evidence.

DV+ does not automatically establish novelty.

## 14. Falsification Conditions

Reduce/reject the candidate target if repeated testing shows that:

1. L3 cannot be distinguished from L1/L2.
2. \(\Delta G\) requires post-hoc interpretation.
3. Relational attribution cannot be separated from independent adaptation.
4. Task₂ performance cannot validly indicate future updateability.
5. Existing frameworks capture the same trajectory with equivalent clarity.
6. RT measurement adds no decision-relevant information beyond current performance and standard adaptation measures.

Negative results are valid architecture-reduction results.

## 15. Pre-Case Freeze Rules

Before opening the outcome of the first selected case:

1. Define A and B.
2. Define T0–T4.
3. Define Task₁ and materially different Task₂.
4. Record baseline evidence for \(G_A(T0)\), \(G_B(T0)\).
5. Specify expected L3 subfunctions.
6. Freeze predicted direction where evidence permits.
7. Freeze alternative explanations.
8. Do not redefine \(G\) after Task₂.
9. Do not infer missing evidence.
10. Preserve asymmetry.
11. Separate measurement success from differential value.
12. Separate differential value from novelty.

## 16. First Validation Objective

The first case asks only:

\[
\boxed{
Can\ we\ distinguish\ and\ observe\
State,\ Behavior,\ Update\ Mechanism,\ and\ Future\ Updateability\
across\ a\ relation\ over\ time?
}
\]

And, if yes:

\[
\boxed{
Does\ that\ relational\ trajectory\
reveal\ something\ socially\ relevant\
that\ current\ performance\ does\ not?
}
\]

## 17. Freeze Statement

**Relational Trajectory of Update Mechanisms** is defined minimally as:

> The longitudinal pattern by which interaction between heterogeneous updating units changes the mechanisms through which one or both units subsequently detect, represent, select, revise, reconfigure, coordinate, verify, or meta-update in response to later change.

This protocol does not assume that the target is novel, measurable, socially valuable, or uniquely captured by IDOS.

Those are empirical questions.

The validation chain is:

\[
\boxed{
Measurement
\rightarrow
Differential\ Value
\rightarrow
Social\ Value
}
\]

not:

\[
Concept
\rightarrow
Novelty\ Claim
\]

**Status: PRE-CASE FREEZE CANDIDATE**
