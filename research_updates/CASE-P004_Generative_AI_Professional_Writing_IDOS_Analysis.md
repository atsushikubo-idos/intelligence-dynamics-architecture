# 1. Stage A — Theory-Neutral Reconstruction

## 1.1 Empirical Setting

The study examined professional writing tasks performed by college-educated professionals.

Participants completed occupationally relevant writing tasks under experimental conditions in which access to ChatGPT was randomized.

The core experimental comparison was therefore:

\[
\text{Professional writing without ChatGPT access}
\]

versus

\[
\text{Professional writing with ChatGPT access}
\]

At this stage, no claim was made that the experiment compared ontologically distinct intelligence systems.

The neutral formulation was:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Human task performance under different tool-access conditions}
}
\]

---

## 1.2 Intervention

The intervention was:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Access to ChatGPT}
}
\]

The treatment did not directly manipulate a defined Human Updating Unit, Human–AI Updating Unit, Reference Frame, Boundary, or Possibility Space.

The most neutral description is:

\[
\text{Human Task Performance}
+
\text{Available Generative-AI Assistance}
\]

The following stronger claims were not assumed:

- AI replaced the Human;
- AI augmented the Human;
- a Human–AI Updating Unit emerged;
- the Human's internal capability changed.

---

## 1.3 Primary Observable Outcomes

The study directly measured task-level outcomes including:

- completion time;
- output quality.

The reported treatment effects were approximately:

\[
\boxed{
Time \downarrow \approx 40\%
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
Quality \uparrow \approx 18\%
}
\]

Thus:

\[
T_{\mathrm{AI}} < T_{\mathrm{Control}}
\]

and:

\[
Q_{\mathrm{AI}} > Q_{\mathrm{Control}}
\]

The strongest theory-neutral statement is:

> Access to ChatGPT changed task-level professional writing performance under the experimental conditions.

---

## 1.4 Heterogeneous Effects

The observed treatment effect was not uniform across participants.

Lower-baseline performers tended to experience larger gains.

Therefore:

\[
\Delta Performance_i
\neq
\Delta Performance_j
\]

The experiment supports heterogeneous treatment effects.

It does not by itself establish:

- skill transfer;
- Human capability convergence;
- Human cognitive updating;
- persistent learning.

# 7. Overall Case Assessment

1. task-level performance improvement is not sufficient evidence of Human Update;
2. Human–AI interaction is not sufficient evidence of Mutual Update;
3. Cross-Unit Influence is not sufficient evidence of Cross-Unit Update;
4. individual evidence cannot automatically be propagated to relational or system levels;
5. strong L1/L2 causal evidence does not automatically become L3/L4 dynamic evidence;
6. several lower-level IDOS constructs are unnecessary for reconstructing this case;
7. the full 12PDM is not empirically recovered;
8. the minimal architecture remains more robust than the richer process vocabulary;
9. existing frameworks remain strong competitors;
10. future IDOS validation should focus increasingly on incremental measurement, prediction, and future Updateability.
