Intelligence Dynamics Architecture
Related Work and Novelty Boundary v1.0

Positioning Intelligence Dynamics Architecture among Existing Research Traditions

1. Purpose of This Document

Intelligence Dynamics Architecture (IDOS) draws on questions that have already been investigated across multiple research traditions.

Many concepts relevant to IDOS have substantial precedents.

These include:

dynamics,
embodiment,
prediction error,
anomaly,
adaptation,
agency,
autonomy,
self-organization,
boundary formation,
frame transformation,
continual learning,
concept drift,
invariance,
interoperability,
and co-evolution.

IDOS therefore does not claim that these concepts were invented within this architecture.

Nor does IDOS claim that the phrase “Intelligence Dynamics” itself is novel.

The purpose of this document is to clarify:

where IDOS overlaps with existing work,
where important differences may exist,
what IDOS does not claim as novel,
and where its candidate contribution may lie.

The novelty boundary described here is provisional.

It should be revised when stronger prior art, criticism, or conceptual overlap is identified.

2. A Conservative Novelty Position

IDOS adopts the following principle:

Novelty should not be claimed for a component merely because it has been expressed in different terminology.

A concept should not be treated as novel if substantially similar structures already exist in another research tradition.

Accordingly, IDOS does not claim novelty for any of the following individually:

intelligence as dynamics,
embodied interaction,
prediction mismatch,
anomaly accumulation,
uncertainty retention,
adaptation,
frame change,
emergent agency,
dynamic boundaries,
trajectory,
invariance,
interoperability,
multi-agent interaction,
or co-evolution.

The candidate contribution of IDOS, if any, must therefore be evaluated at the level of architecture, integration, relations among concepts, operationalization, and explanatory usefulness.

3. Prior Use of “Intelligence Dynamics”

Masahiro Fujita proposed Intelligence Dynamics in work published in 2009 on open-ended learning agents.

That work emphasized:

dynamics,
embodiment,
interaction with the real world,
developmental intelligence,
attractor dynamics,
intrinsic motivation,
and open-ended learning.

Fujita's work is therefore an important prior use of both the phrase Intelligence Dynamics and a dynamical approach to intelligence.

IDOS does not claim priority for either.

A preliminary distinction is that Fujita's formulation begins from embodied agents interacting with the world and investigates developmental intelligence through dynamical processes.

IDOS asks a different architectural question:

What happens if neither the Agent nor even the Trajectory is assumed to be permanently fixed?

IDOS investigates whether:

Reality Contact
→ Difference
→ Residual
→ Holding
→ Update
→ Trajectory

can be used to trace dynamics from which:

Updating Unit
Boundary
Reference Frame
Agency

may themselves emerge, transform, dissolve, or re-emerge.

This distinction should be treated as a candidate architectural difference rather than a claim of theoretical superiority.

4. Predictive Processing and Prediction Error

Predictive approaches to cognition emphasize the importance of mismatch between prediction and incoming information.

This creates an obvious relation to the IDOS concept of Residual.

However:

Residual should not be treated as a renamed prediction error.

Within IDOS, Residual is intended as a broader architectural category.

It may refer to a Difference that cannot be sufficiently absorbed by the currently operative:

representation,
expectation,
Reference Frame,
Boundary,
organization,
or updating process.

IDOS also explicitly allows Residual to remain unresolved through Holding.

The relevant research question is therefore not whether prediction error exists.

It is whether the broader distinction:

Difference → Residual → Holding → Update

provides explanatory or operational value beyond existing error-based formulations.

If it does not, the architecture should be reduced accordingly.

5. Active Inference and the Free Energy Principle

Active Inference and the Free Energy Principle provide sophisticated accounts of perception, action, inference, and self-organization.

Work involving Markov blankets is particularly relevant to IDOS because it addresses statistical boundaries between internal and external states and allows nested forms of organization.

This overlaps substantially with IDOS concerns involving:

Boundary,
self-organization,
interaction,
hierarchical organization,
and relations between system and environment.

IDOS therefore does not claim that dynamic or emergent boundaries are novel.

The candidate distinction is different.

IDOS treats Boundary as one component within a broader trajectory-centered architecture in which:

Trajectory
Updating Unit
Boundary
Reference Frame
Possibility Space

may all become variables of transformation.

Whether this broader organization provides anything not already expressible within Active Inference remains an open comparison problem.

6. Enactivism and Autopoiesis

Enactivist and autopoietic traditions have long challenged the idea that cognition should be understood as passive representation inside a predefined agent.

Relevant themes include:

autonomy,
embodiment,
sense-making,
self-production,
individuation,
organism-environment relations,
and emergent Agency.

Some enactive accounts explicitly treat self-individuation as an open and unfinished process unfolding over time.

This is closely related to IDOS concerns.

Therefore IDOS does not claim novelty for:

Agency as emergent

or:

the individual as dynamically constituted.

The candidate IDOS contribution lies instead in connecting these concerns to an explicit architecture of:

Residual
Holding
Update
Trajectory
Coupling
CDP
Interoperability
Governance

across heterogeneous biological, artificial, organizational, and social systems.

Whether this integration constitutes a useful new architecture remains to be demonstrated.

7. Cybernetics and Second-Order Cybernetics

Cybernetics provides major precedents for thinking about:

feedback,
regulation,
adaptation,
control,
circular causality,
observer-system relations,
and recursive organization.

Second-order cybernetics further problematizes the position of the observer.

IDOS shares this recursive orientation.

In particular:

Reality → Difference → Update → Action → Reality Re-contact

is structurally related to feedback-oriented traditions.

IDOS does not claim novelty for recursive feedback.

Its candidate distinction is the attempt to connect recursive dynamics to:

Residual preservation,
Holding,
Reference Frame transformation,
Trajectory history,
dynamic Updating Units,
changing Possibility Spaces,
heterogeneous interoperability,
and trajectory-level governance.
8. Kuhn and Paradigm Change

Thomas Kuhn's work on scientific revolutions provides an important precedent for the relation among:

normal activity → anomaly → crisis → paradigm transformation

This has an obvious resemblance to IDOS discussions of:

Residual accumulation → deeper Update → Frame Transformation

IDOS therefore does not claim that persistent anomalies leading to changes in interpretive structure are a new idea.

The scope is different.

Kuhn primarily analyzed scientific development and paradigm change.

IDOS asks whether structurally related dynamics can be operationally compared across:

humans,
AI systems,
organizations,
Human–AI configurations,
and other Updating Units.

Whether such generalization is legitimate is itself an empirical question.

9. Transformative Learning

Transformative Learning research investigates changes in the frames through which people interpret experience.

This overlaps with IDOS concepts such as:

Reference Frame,
disorienting experience,
reflection,
and Frame Transformation.

IDOS therefore does not claim novelty for the idea that learning may transform the frame through which experience is interpreted.

The candidate distinction lies in placing frame transformation inside a broader cross-system architecture involving:

Residual → Holding → Update → Trajectory

and extending the question beyond individual human learning.

10. Continual Learning

Continual Learning studies systems that acquire and adapt knowledge over time while addressing problems such as catastrophic forgetting.

This overlaps with IDOS concerns involving:

historical continuity,
adaptation,
preservation,
learning across time,
and Updateability.

IDOS does not claim novelty for lifelong or continual adaptation.

A potentially relevant distinction is:

Update without Erasure

Within IDOS, preservation is not limited to preventing parameter forgetting.

The question may also concern preservation or loss of:

provenance,
alternative Reference Frames,
minority trajectories,
Boundary histories,
unresolved Residuals,
and future Possibility Spaces.

Whether these broader preservation concerns can be operationalized remains open.

11. Concept Drift

Concept Drift research studies environments in which statistical relationships change over time and learning systems must adapt.

This overlaps directly with the IDOS concern that:

the environment and the conditions of interpretation are not necessarily stationary.

IDOS does not claim novelty for adaptation to changing environments.

The candidate extension is that IDOS allows not only the learned mapping but also:

Reference Frame,
Boundary,
Updating Unit,
Trajectory,
and Possibility Space

to become objects of possible transformation.

Again, whether these distinctions provide measurable value beyond existing adaptive-learning approaches must be tested.

12. Double-Loop Learning and Organizational Learning

Organizational learning research distinguishes between correcting actions within existing assumptions and revising the assumptions or governing variables themselves.

This strongly resembles the distinction between shallow and deeper forms of Update in IDOS.

Therefore IDOS does not claim novelty for the distinction:

parameter-level correction ≠ frame-level transformation

The L1–L5 model should be understood as a provisional attempt to extend and operationalize update depth across heterogeneous systems, not as the first recognition that learning can occur at different structural levels.

13. Invariance and Structural Continuity

Multiple research traditions investigate structures that remain stable across changing environments, representations, or theories.

These include work on:

invariance,
causal structure,
structural realism,
transfer,
and out-of-distribution generalization.

Invariant Risk Minimization, for example, explicitly seeks invariant correlations across multiple training distributions.

IDOS therefore does not claim novelty for searching for invariance.

Its formulation is deliberately weaker:

Invariant Search ≠ Invariant Assumption

IDOS asks whether some structures remain sufficiently robust across specified transformations involving:

State,
Reference Frame,
Boundary,
scale,
observer,
environment,
or Trajectory.

An Invariant Candidate remains a candidate until tested.

Furthermore:

Observed Persistence ≠ Normative Preservation

Even if something persists, that does not establish that it should be preserved.

14. Multi-Agent Systems and Interoperability

Multi-agent research already addresses:

communication,
coordination,
heterogeneous agents,
distributed decision-making,
protocols,
shared ontologies,
negotiation,
and interoperability.

IDOS therefore does not claim novelty for heterogeneous-agent communication or interoperability.

The CDP proposal is narrower and more specific.

It asks whether heterogeneous Updating Units can interoperate through descriptions of their dynamics of change without requiring complete agreement about their internal representations.

Conceptually:

Internal A ≠ Internal B

while potentially:

CDP Description A ↔ CDP Description B

The relevant question is whether shared descriptions of:

Residual,
Holding,
Update,
Trajectory,
Boundary,
Reference Frame,
Possibility Space,
and provenance

provide useful interoperability not already achieved by existing approaches.

This remains unproven.

15. Co-evolution and Complex Systems

Complex systems, evolutionary theory, ecology, social systems research, and related fields already investigate co-evolution.

IDOS does not claim novelty for the idea that interacting systems change one another.

Within IDOS:

(A(t), B(t), Relation(t))
→
(A(t+1), B(t+1), Relation(t+1))

is simply a conceptual representation of mutual change.

The candidate architectural proposition is that:

Updateability and Interoperability may jointly support forms of Co-evolution among heterogeneous Updating Units.

This is not presented as a mathematical law.

It is a research proposition.

Furthermore:

Co-evolution ≠ Desirable Evolution

Mutual change may produce:

dependency,
lock-in,
power concentration,
loss of Agency,
narrowing of Possibility Space,
or irreversible structural change.

Therefore co-evolution requires governance rather than automatic celebration.

17. Candidate Distinctiveness of IDOS

The candidate distinctiveness of IDOS does not lie in any single concept.

It lies, if anywhere, in a particular integration.

The current candidate architecture begins with:

Reality Contact
→ Observation
→ Difference
→ Residual
→ Holding
→ Update
→ Trajectory

It then asks whether interactions among trajectories can produce:

Multiple Trajectories
→ Coupling
→ Enactment
→ Updating Unit

while allowing:

Updating Unit
Boundary
Reference Frame
Agency
Possibility Space

to remain dynamically revisable.

It further asks whether heterogeneous Updating Units can become interoperable through:

CDP
→ Description
→ Translation
→ Comparison
→ Coordination

without requiring:

Representational Unification

or:

Forced Value Alignment

and whether these interactions can be studied at the level of:

Co-evolution
→ Intelligence Ecology
→ Governance

The resulting architecture is:

Trajectory-centered, but not trajectory-fixed.

Neither Agent nor Trajectory is assumed to be permanently given.

18. A More Precise Candidate Contribution

The current candidate contribution can be stated as follows:

The candidate distinctiveness of IDOS does not lie in any single concept such as dynamics, emergence, coupling, agency, boundary formation, interoperability, or trajectory. Closely related ideas exist across enactivism, autopoiesis, active inference, cybernetics, continual learning, multi-agent systems, and dynamical approaches to intelligence.

Its proposed contribution lies in their integration into a trajectory-centered architecture that begins with Reality contact and traces Difference, Residual, Holding, and Update as histories of Trajectory; investigates the emergence of Updating Units, Boundaries, Reference Frames, and Agency through the coupling, transition, and branching of trajectories; and explores whether heterogeneous Updating Units can interoperate and co-evolve through a Common Dynamics Protocol without requiring the unification of their internal representations or forced value alignment.

This statement is a candidate novelty claim.

It is not a declaration of established novelty.

19. Trajectory-Centered, but Not Trajectory-Fixed

One possible point of differentiation requires particular clarification.

Moving from:

Agent → Trajectory

is not sufficient.

Trajectory itself must not become a replacement fixed ontology.

IDOS therefore proposes:

Trajectory-centered, but not trajectory-fixed.

Trajectories may:

emerge,
persist,
couple,
branch,
transition,
become dormant,
dissolve,
and re-emerge.

This produces an additional research problem:

What makes a transformed process the same Trajectory across time?

IDOS currently has no universal answer.

Candidate approaches involving:

provenance,
causal continuity,
structural continuity,
historical dependence,
retained constraints,
and relational continuity

remain open.

20. The Role of Holding

Holding may become an important point of differentiation, but novelty should not yet be claimed.

Related ideas exist in:

uncertainty retention,
delayed commitment,
ambiguity tolerance,
negative capability,
hypothesis maintenance,
and other traditions.

The candidate IDOS contribution is therefore not the invention of unresolved-state preservation.

It is the structural placement of Holding within:

Residual → Holding → Update → Trajectory

and potentially:

Residual → Holding → Frame Transformation / Possibility-Space Transformation

Whether this structural role provides explanatory or practical value should be tested.

21. The Role of CDP

CDP may also become a distinctive component, but it remains provisional.

Its central research question is:

Can heterogeneous intelligences remain interoperable and co-evolve without requiring representational unification or forced value alignment?

CDP proposes that interoperability may operate through descriptions of dynamic change rather than through complete internal equivalence.

However, existing work in:

multi-agent communication,
ontology alignment,
distributed systems,
agent communication languages,
semantic interoperability,
and human–machine coordination

must be compared carefully before any strong novelty claim is made.

22. The Role of Reality

IDOS repeatedly returns its descriptions to Reality.

This should not be interpreted as a claim of unmediated realism.

Reality contact is always potentially mediated by:

observation,
embodiment,
instrumentation,
language,
institution,
history,
Boundary,
and Reference Frame.

The architectural function of Reality is instead to prevent the system from becoming completely self-referential.

Conceptually:

Reality → Difference → Residual → Update → Action → Reality Re-contact

No representation, theory, protocol, or Reference Frame is treated as equivalent to Reality itself.

This includes IDOS.

23. Alternative Philosophical Reference Frames

IDOS may also explore intellectual traditions outside dominant computational approaches to intelligence.

Potential reference frames include concepts from:

Buddhist dependent origination,
impermanence,
non-fixed self,
relational ontology,
and other traditions concerned with process and relation.

These are not presented as scientific evidence for IDOS.

They are alternative Reference Frames that may expose assumptions hidden within other formulations.

Philosophical analogy should not be confused with empirical validation.

24. Integration Is Not Validation

An architecture can integrate many existing ideas and still be:

redundant,
unnecessary,
untestable,
poorly operationalized,
or less useful than existing theories.

Therefore:

Integration ≠ Novelty

and:

Novelty ≠ Validity

and:

Internal Consistency ≠ Evidence

IDOS must ultimately be evaluated by external criticism, implementation, comparison, and empirical work.

25. Conditions Under Which the Novelty Claim Should Shrink

The candidate novelty boundary should be narrowed if prior work demonstrates that another framework already provides substantially the same architecture.

IDOS should also shrink if:

Residual adds nothing beyond existing error concepts,
Holding adds nothing beyond existing uncertainty mechanisms,
Trajectory adds nothing beyond existing historical state representations,
Updating Unit adds nothing beyond existing individuation concepts,
CDP adds nothing beyond existing interoperability frameworks,
Possibility-Space Transformation cannot be distinguished from recombination,
or trajectory-level governance adds no useful information beyond existing monitoring approaches.

The appropriate response to such findings is not to defend terminology.

It is to update the architecture.

The architecture must be allowed to shrink.

26. Research Program Rather Than Priority Claim

Canonical v1.0 should therefore be understood primarily as:

a public research architecture

rather than:

a priority claim over its individual components.

The purpose of publication is to make the architecture:

visible,
inspectable,
criticizable,
comparable,
implementable,
falsifiable,
and revisable.

The strongest future contribution may not be that IDOS invented a new concept.

It may instead be that the architecture provides a useful common structure through which previously separated research traditions, technologies, practices, and governance problems can be connected and tested together.

Whether it succeeds in doing so remains open.

28. Open Invitation

This document is intentionally incomplete.

Researchers and practitioners are invited to identify:

overlooked prior art,
equivalent formulations,
unnecessary terminology,
stronger existing models,
conceptual contradictions,
failed distinctions,
and empirical counterexamples.

A finding that an IDOS component already exists elsewhere is not treated as a threat to the project.

It is useful information about the actual novelty boundary.

Novelty Boundary itself is updateable.

Closing

IDOS does not begin from the assumption that it has discovered a new universal theory of intelligence.

It begins from a different objective:

to build an open architecture in which existing and emerging insights about intelligence, updating, transformation, interoperability, and governance can be connected without prematurely forcing them into one fixed ontology.

Its candidate contribution is therefore architectural and integrative.

That contribution remains subject to comparison with prior work.

Nothing in this document establishes scientific priority.

Nothing in this document establishes scientific validity.

Both must remain open to external evidence and criticism.
