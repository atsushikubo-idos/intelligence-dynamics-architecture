# 1. Case Title

**Microsoft Security Copilot — Randomized Controlled Trial**

---

# 2. Regime Classification

$$
\boxed{V001 = Present\ Normal}
$$

V001 is used as the low-complexity baseline of the five-case pilot.

The case is characterized by:

- AI functioning primarily as a support system;
- recognizable human operational authority;
- relatively stable organizational boundaries;
- conventional performance measurement;
- limited autonomous AI-to-AI interaction;
- relatively strong external reality contact.

The purpose of V001 is not to demonstrate IDOS strength.

It is deliberately used to test whether IDOS adds little under relatively stable present-day Human–AI conditions.

# 4. Stage 1 — Raw Reconstruction

## 4.1 What happened?

Microsoft conducted randomized controlled trials to evaluate productivity gains from Copilot for Security.

External experimental subjects logged into a Microsoft 365 Defender environment and completed four security-related tasks:

- Incident Summarization
- Script Analyzer
- Incident Report
- Guided Response

Some participants were randomly given access to Copilot for Security and others were not.

The AI system supported users by:

- interpreting security incidents;
- explaining attack scripts;
- organizing incident facts;
- suggesting remediation or response steps.

The observed experimental structure was approximately:

$$
Security\ Data
\rightarrow
AI\ Interpretation
\rightarrow
Human\ Analyst
\rightarrow
Security\ Judgment / Response
$$

The system was not evaluated as a fully autonomous security operator in this experiment.

---

## 4.2 Participants

Microsoft reported a novice study involving **149 participants** recruited externally.

These participants had general IT capability but limited specialized cybersecurity expertise.

The published study also reports improvements for both novices and security professionals, although the novice cohort provides the clearest documented baseline for the initial productivity comparison.

---

## 4.3 Observed outcomes

The study reported improvements in speed and accuracy for Copilot users.

For the novice group, Microsoft reported that across three multiple-choice tasks, participants with Copilot answered approximately **35% more questions correctly** overall.

Reported task-level accuracy improvements included approximately:

- Script Analyzer: **34%**
- Incident Report: **25%**
- Guided Response: **43%**

Microsoft also explicitly cautioned that:

- the experimental tasks were closely aligned with Copilot capabilities;
- novice baselines were relatively low;
- real-world effects may therefore be smaller.

---

## 4.4 What was measured?

The experimental measurement focused mainly on:

- task completion time;
- accuracy;
- answer quality;
- task-level productivity.

Conceptually:

$$
Measured\ Success
\approx
Speed + Accuracy + Task\ Quality
$$

---

## 4.5 What was not directly measured?

The published RCT does not directly establish:

- long-term independent cybersecurity skill formation;
- human capability after Copilot removal;
- long-term AI dependency;
- organizational restructuring caused by AI use;
- changes in decision authority;
- long-run fallback capacity;
- whether user corrections materially update the underlying model;
- changes in the organization's future ability to redesign its own AI-dependent workflow.

These are not identified as observed failures.

They are simply outside the main scope of the experiment.

---

## 4.6 Raw actors

The directly relevant actors and components include:

- human security analyst;
- Microsoft Security Copilot;
- Microsoft 365 Defender;
- security incident data;
- organizational security workflow.

---

## 4.7 Raw decision structure

The basic experimental structure can be summarized as:

$$
AI\ proposes / interprets
$$

$$
Human\ evaluates
$$

$$
Human\ acts
$$

Human responsibility and task execution remain recognizable.

# 5. Stage 2 — Conventional Analysis

## 5.1 Conventional explanation

The case can be explained strongly using established analytical frameworks.

The simplest explanation is:

$$
\boxed{AI = Cognitive\ Assistance}
$$

Copilot reduces portions of:

$$
Search
+
Interpretation
+
Recall
+
Synthesis
$$

This can yield:

$$
Cognitive\ Load \downarrow
$$

$$
Search\ Cost \downarrow
$$

$$
Task\ Completion\ Time \downarrow
$$

$$
Accuracy \uparrow
$$

---

## 5.2 Decision support

The system is readily interpretable as an advanced decision-support system:

$$
Information
\rightarrow
Recommendation
\rightarrow
Human\ Judgment
$$

No distinct IDOS concept is required to explain this basic structure.

---

## 5.3 Human–AI teaming

The observed result can also be interpreted through Human–AI teaming.

AI contributes:

- rapid information processing;
- summarization;
- pattern recognition;
- retrieval and synthesis.

Humans contribute:

- contextual judgment;
- exception handling;
- accountability;
- final interpretation.

Thus:

$$
Human + AI > Human\ Alone
$$

is a conventional and sufficient explanation for much of the short-run result.

---

## 5.4 Skill augmentation

The larger gains among novices can be interpreted through:

- skill augmentation;
- expertise theory;
- learning curves;
- knowledge substitution.

Novices have less accumulated expertise and therefore more room to benefit from AI assistance.

---

## 5.5 Knowledge management

Copilot can also be viewed as a mechanism for making expert or organizational knowledge more accessible:

$$
Organizational\ Knowledge
\rightarrow
AI
\rightarrow
Individual\ Worker
$$

This fits conventional knowledge-management analysis.

---

## 5.6 Existing risk frameworks

Potential longer-term risks are also already addressed by established fields:

- automation bias;
- overreliance;
- deskilling;
- resilience loss;
- lock-in;
- governance;
- accountability;
- measurement bias.

These should not be counted as uniquely IDOS-derived findings.

---

## 5.7 Conventional blind spots

The remaining difficulty is not that existing theory cannot address the relevant questions.

Rather, the issues are often fragmented across different analytical traditions.

Examples include:

### A. Who is actually learning?

Is observed improvement located in:

$$
Human
$$

or:

$$
Human + AI
$$

or:

$$
Organization
$$

?

### B. Performance vs capability

It is possible in principle that:

$$
Performance_t \uparrow
$$

while:

$$
Independent\ Capability_{t+1} \downarrow
$$

### C. Verification vs system revision

A user may be able to inspect or reject AI output without being able to alter the underlying system.

### D. Dependency trajectory

Short-run augmentation may later become structural dependency.

### E. Measurement lock-in

Current success metrics may fail to capture future system capability.

---

## 5.8 Conventional analysis conclusion

$$
\boxed{
Conventional\ Analysis\ is\ already\ strong
}
$$

At this stage:

$$
\boxed{
No\ clear\ IDOS\ necessity\ yet
}
$$

# 6. Stage 3 — IDOS Analysis

## 6.1 Updating Unit

The experiment directly demonstrates improved performance of a coupled Human–AI task system.

It does not directly demonstrate increased independent human capability.

Thus:

$$
U_0 = Human
$$

may partially shift toward:

$$
U_1 = Human\text{-}AI\ Task\ System
$$

A conservative interpretation is:

$$
\boxed{
Updating\ Unit = Human\text{-}centered\ coupled\ unit
}
$$

This makes explicit the distinction:

$$
Performance_{Human+AI}\uparrow
$$

does not automatically imply:

$$
Capability_{Human}\uparrow
$$

---

## 6.2 Dynamic Boundary

AI enters the operational work process:

$$
Human
\leftrightarrow
Copilot
\leftrightarrow
Security\ Data
$$

However, authority, responsibility, and organizational structure remain comparatively stable.

Thus:

$$
B_t \approx stable
$$

V001 is better characterized as:

$$
Boundary\ expansion
$$

rather than major boundary migration.

---

## 6.3 Trajectory

The RCT mainly measures short-run performance.

IDOS adds a longitudinal question:

$$
Current\ Performance
\neq
Future\ Trajectory
$$

Relevant future variables may include:

- independent human skill;
- AI dependency;
- fallback capacity;
- model dependence;
- ability to redesign the workflow.

The key contribution is not the idea of deskilling itself.

It is the ability to connect multiple future consequences into one longitudinal update structure.

---

## 6.4 Difference

Potential differences include:

$$
Human\ Interpretation
\neq
AI\ Recommendation
$$

and:

$$
Observed\ Incident
\neq
Expected\ State
$$

However, anomaly detection and disagreement can already be treated conventionally.

Difference is therefore not central in V001.

---

## 6.5 Reference Frame

Human and AI interpretation may rely on different frames:

$$
F_H \neq F_{AI}
$$

But the RCT does not directly measure frame divergence.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Frame\ divergence = plausible,\ not\ demonstrated
}
$$

---

## 6.6 Measurement

The experiment measures mainly speed, accuracy, and task quality.

IDOS asks whether:

$$
Measured\ Success
$$

is equivalent to:

$$
System\ Capability
$$

A system can improve measured task performance while unmeasured capabilities change in another direction.

This does not constitute a unique discovery, but it becomes integrated into the same system trajectory.

---

## 6.7 Provenance

Later system revision may depend on preserving:

- what the AI generated;
- what evidence was used;
- what the human changed;
- what decision was ultimately taken.

Conceptually:

$$
Provenance
\rightarrow
Later\ Revisability
$$

However, the RCT does not demonstrate provenance failure.

---

## 6.8 Future Updateability

Working definition:

$$
\boxed{
Future\ Updateability
=
Capacity\ to\ revise\ the\ conditions\ of\ future\ revision
}
$$

The relevant question is not merely:

$$
Can\ the\ system\ perform?
$$

but:

$$
Can\ the\ system\ later\ change\ how\ it\ performs?
$$

A theoretically possible trajectory is:

$$
Performance_t \uparrow
$$

while:

$$
Future\ Updateability_t \downarrow
$$

if increasing AI dependence reduces the organization's ability to alter:

- decision rules;
- human expertise;
- system architecture;
- model dependence;
- measurement regimes;
- fallback procedures.

The V001 evidence does not show that this decline occurred.

It only shows that short-run performance improvement is insufficient evidence that Future Updateability was preserved.

---

## 6.9 CFV

Human users can inspect AI outputs, but:

$$
Output\ Visibility
\neq
Verifiability
$$

True verification depends on access to evidence, context, and sufficiently interpretable output.

In V001, CFV is useful but not uniquely necessary.

---

## 6.10 MU

The update relation may be asymmetric.

AI recommendations can alter human judgment:

$$
AI \rightarrow Human
$$

But human correction does not necessarily modify the underlying model:

$$
Human \not\Rightarrow Immediate\ Model\ Update
$$

Thus:

$$
MU_{AI\rightarrow H}
\neq
MU_{H\rightarrow AI}
$$

This is useful for separating interaction from actual system revision.

---

## 6.11 Verification ≠ Influence ≠ Update

A human may:

1. inspect an AI output;
2. accept or reject it;
3. still lack the power to modify the underlying AI system.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Verification
\neq
Influence
\neq
Update
}
$$

---

## 6.12 Power

Power is structurally relevant to:

- system selection;
- model modification;
- shutdown authority;
- measurement design;
- update authority.

However, the V001 evidence base does not provide enough detail to make Power a central observed finding.

---

## 6.13 Temporal Compatibility

The experiment preserves time for human review.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Temporal\ Compatibility \approx preserved
}
$$

This provides a useful baseline for later regimes.

---

## 6.14 Reality Contact

Cybersecurity outcomes remain linked to external events and operational consequences.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Reality\ Contact = relatively\ strong
}
$$

Again, this is one reason V001 does not strongly activate the full IDOS architecture.

---

## 6.15 Optional / extended concepts

### Residual

Not necessary for the principal V001 insight.

### Holding

Not materially required.

### Possibility Space

Potentially useful, but unnecessary for the core analysis.

### 12PDM

High-resolution 12PDM analysis is not required for V001.

# 10. Open Residuals

The following questions remain open and should not be resolved from this case alone:

1. Does long-term AI assistance actually reduce independent human security capability?
2. Does AI dependency reduce Future Updateability in real organizations?
3. At what level does the effective Updating Unit shift from human-centered to coupled or system-level?
4. When does MU asymmetry become materially consequential?
5. At what regime does Dynamic Boundary become N2 rather than N0?
6. Does Temporal Compatibility become a decisive factor in more autonomous regimes?

These questions should be carried forward to V002–V005 rather than used to expand the theory immediately.

# 12. Freeze Rule

This V001 result should now be treated as frozen for the five-case pilot.

It should not be revised merely because later cases produce stronger or weaker IDOS results.

Permissible later changes are limited to:

- factual correction;
